# 3. Food

## The staple is "stiff cornmeal porridge"

From East to Southern Africa, the staple is a white, firm, rice-cake-like food made by stirring cornmeal into boiling water. Its name changes by country.

| Country | Name |
| --- | --- |
| Kenya, Tanzania | **Ugali** |
| Malawi, Zambia | **Nsima** |
| Zimbabwe | Sadza |
| South Africa | Pap |

- With your right hand, pull off a bite-sized piece, roll it into a ball, press a dip into it with your fingers, and scoop up the side dish with it
- It has almost no taste of its own; it is eaten with side dishes to fill your stomach. Many people say "if I haven't had ugali (nsima), I don't feel like I've eaten"
- That is why the corn harvest and its price are directly tied to daily life and politics (Chapters 6–8)

Other staples

- Rice (pilau and biryani; common in coastal towns)
- Chapati (a thin flatbread that came from India)
- Cassava, sweet potatoes
- Matoke (cooking bananas; in Uganda and northwestern Tanzania)

## Everyday side dishes

| Dish | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Sukuma wiki | Leafy greens like kale, simmered with onions and tomatoes. The name means "push the week" (cheap enough to eat every day) |
| Maharage | Bean stew. Sometimes cooked in coconut milk |
| Nyama choma | Meat (goat, beef) grilled over charcoal. A treat for weekends and gatherings |
| Samaki | Fish. Tilapia from Lake Victoria and others, fried or stewed |
| Kachumbari | A fresh salad of tomatoes, onions and chili |

## Specialties of the three countries

### Tanzania

- **Chipsi mayai**: an omelet with French fries cooked into the egg. A street-stall classic
- **Mishkaki**: grilled beef skewers
- **Pilau**: rice cooked with spices (coastal food)
- **Urojo** (Zanzibar mix): a sour, spicy soup from Zanzibar's street stalls
- Zanzibar is famous for spices such as cloves, and is called the "Spice Island"

### Kenya

- **Githeri**: corn and beans cooked together (a dish of the Kikuyu people)
- **Mukimo**: mashed potatoes, greens, corn and beans
- **Mandazi**: fried bread. Eaten with morning tea
- **Mursik**: fermented milk of the Kalenjin people. The inside of a gourd is smoked with charcoal, then milk is put in and fermented. Known as a food of long-distance runners

### Malawi

- **Chambo**: a kind of tilapia from Lake Malawi. Malawi's most popular fish, eaten with nsima
- **Usipa**: small fish from Lake Malawi. Sold dried
- **Kondowole**: nsima made from cassava flour (eaten on the lakeshore in the north)
- **Thobwa**: a sweet and sour drink of lightly fermented corn or sorghum (not alcoholic)
- Many dishes cook greens with ground peanuts

## Drinks

- **Chai**: East African tea is a sweet milk tea made by boiling water, milk, tea leaves and sugar together. Ginger (*tangawizi* in Swahili) is sometimes added. Kenya is one of the world's leading tea exporters
- **Coffee**: the home of the coffee plant is Ethiopia. Tanzania's Kilimanjaro coffee is famous in Japan too, but locally tea is drunk more
- **Milk**: in Kenya, milk is an everyday drink. It is boiled before drinking. Fermented milk (*maziwa lala* in Swahili, "milk that has slept") is also popular
- **Beer**: Tusker (Kenya), Kilimanjaro and Safari (Tanzania), Carlsberg (which has a brewery in Malawi). There is also a sour, thick drink made from sorghum or corn (Chibuku)

## Table manners

- Wash your hands before eating. At homes and eateries, someone may bring a basin and a jug and pour water over your hands
- Eat and pass things with your **right hand**. The left hand is considered unclean
- It is polite to taste at least a little of any food or drink you are offered
- If you are invited to someone's home, gifts such as sugar, tea leaves or fruit are appreciated

## Food today

- In towns, supermarkets, fast food and cafes are increasing (such as the Nairobi cafe chain "Java House")
- Even so, most food is bought at markets and small shops (*duka*, kiosks), just as much as is needed for that day
- Many homes have no refrigerator, so milk is drunk the same day or made into fermented milk. This connects to the "cooling milk" story in Chapters 6–8
- Child malnutrition (stunting, when children do not grow tall enough) is a big problem in Tanzania and Malawi. There is demand for delivering protein such as milk, eggs and fish cheaply
