## The country at a glance

![Where Kenya is](images/kenya-map.jpg)

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Capital | Nairobi (about 4.4 million, 2019). The port city is Mombasa |
| Population | About 47.6 million (2019 census). Now over 50 million |
| Languages | English and Swahili (both official). Young people in Nairobi speak "Sheng", a slang that mixes the two |
| Currency | Kenyan shilling (KES) |
| President | William Ruto (2022–). The next election is in August 2027 |
| Mobile operators | Safaricom (M-Pesa; the largest), Airtel |
| Groups it belongs to | EAC, COMESA |

- The center of East Africa's economy, transport and finance. Many UN agencies and foreign companies have their eastern Africa headquarters in Nairobi
- It has so many startups that it is called "**Silicon Savannah**", and in 2025 its startups raised the most money in Africa (about 1 billion dollars)
- **82%** of adults use mobile money (2024), and **84.8%** use financial services such as banks
- About 90% of its electricity comes from renewable sources such as geothermal, hydro and wind
- The country is divided into 47 counties, and **agriculture is the counties' responsibility**. Subsidies and rules differ by county
- In June 2024, young people protesting a tax bill broke into parliament, and the bill was withdrawn. Protests and crackdowns have continued since

## Agriculture

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Share of GDP | About 22% (2024) |
| Top export earner | Horticulture (flowers, vegetables, fruit), about 203.6 billion shillings (2024) |
| Second export | Tea, about 189.1 billion shillings (2024). One of the world's leading tea exporters |
| Other crops | Coffee, avocados, macadamia, corn (the staple; shortfalls are imported) |

- Flowers (roses and others) are grown in large greenhouses around Lake Naivasha and flown to Europe every night
- Tea is grown in the highlands of Kericho and Nyeri; leaves picked by small farmers are processed in factories owned by the farmers as shareholders (managed by KTDA)
- Since 2022, the government has sold fertilizer cheaply to registered farmers (registration and redemption are done by phone)
- The dry north is a herding region with cattle, goats and camels. In the great drought of 2020–23, livestock died in huge numbers

## Dairy

Kenyan dairy is the most commercialized in East Africa. Milk is an everyday drink, and consumption per person is among the highest in East Africa.

### Numbers

| Item | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| Milk produced | About 5.2 billion L a year (Kenya Dairy Board, KDB, estimate; includes milk that does not go through factories) |
| Milk bought from farmers by factories | About 910 million L (2024; a record, up 12% from the year before) |
| Milk that does not go through factories | About 80% (the informal market) |
| Small-scale dairy farmers | More than 1.8 million households |
| Milk per cow per day | About 10 L on average (2024; 6–7 L in 2021) |
| Number of cattle (2019 census) | About 2.2 million dairy cattle, about 13 million native cattle |
| Price farmers sell at | About 45–55 shillings per L (2025). The cost of production is 30–37 shillings per L (KDB) |

### Cattle and farmers

- The producing areas are the central highlands (Kiambu, Nyandarua, Nyeri, Murang'a), the Rift Valley (Nakuru, Uasin Gishu, Nandi, Bomet) and Meru
- Most are family farms with 1–3 cows. Holstein (Friesian), Ayrshire, Jersey and their crosses
- Land is scarce, so "**zero-grazing**" (no grazing) is common: cows are kept in a shed and cut grass (such as Napier grass) is carried to them
- Artificial insemination is widespread, and the government and counties subsidize the fees
- In towns there are vending machines that sell milk by volume (**milk ATMs**), where people come with their own containers

![Milking by hand (Murang'a, central Kenya)](images/milking-kenya.jpg)

### Dairies and cooperatives

- Farmers' milk is gathered at **cooperative** collection centers, cooled, and taken to factories
- Large dairies: Brookside (the largest private one), New KCC (state-owned), Githunguri (a cooperative; the "Fresha" brand), Bio Food, Daima and others
- Cooperatives in Meru, Murang'a and elsewhere own their own factories and feed mills

![Milk cans lined up at a cooperative collection center (Kiambu County)](images/milk-cans-kiambaa.jpg)

![A child drinking milk (Kenya)](images/kids-milk.jpg)

### Problems

- **Price swings**: in the rainy season there is surplus milk and prices fall; in the dry season there is a shortage and prices rise
- **Feed**: compound feed is expensive. Grass runs short in the dry season. Contractors who make silage for farmers are appearing
- **Quality**: milk that does not go through factories is sold without testing. Problems include adding water and antibiotic residues
- **Disease**: East Coast fever (ECF), mastitis
- **Trade with neighbors**: competition from cheap Ugandan dairy products, and Tanzania's tax on Kenyan milk (2025)

## IoT and agritech companies

### Dairy

| Company | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| [**Savanna Circuit Technologies**](https://sav-circuit.com/) | A solar milk cooler mounted on a motorbike (keeps 140 L cold for 15 hours). Rented rather than sold, charging per liter carried ("cooling as a service"). More than 1,000 units in 26 counties (company figures) |
| **FRESH Networks** | Refrigerated delivery with IoT temperature monitoring, and milk ATMs for low-income areas |
| [**DigiCow**](https://digicow.co.ke/) | Record-keeping app, training and loans for dairy farmers. Records can be kept without internet, and it also works by text message |
| [**Dairy Sense**](https://dairysense.tech/) | A portable milk tester for use at farms and collection centers. Building a system that sets prices by quality |
| **SpaceAI** | Manages small dairy farmers' milk data with AI, from collection to factory |

### Crops, money and communications

| Company | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| [**SunCulture**](https://sunculture.io/) | Sells solar irrigation pumps on installment plans. Monitors pump status remotely |
| [**UjuziKilimo**](https://www.ujuzikilimo.com/) | Uses soil sensors to check soil conditions and recommend fertilizer and work |
| [**Synnefa**](https://synnefa.io/) | Greenhouse and field sensors, and a cloud service for farmers |
| [**Apollo Agriculture**](https://www.apolloagriculture.com/) | Screens farmers using satellite images and machine learning, and sells seed and fertilizer on credit |
| [**Pula**](https://www.pula-advisors.com/) | Insurance for farmers using weather and yield data |
| [**Safaricom**](https://www.safaricom.co.ke/) | M-Pesa. "DigiFarm" for farmers (advance payments to tea farmers, advice through an AI chat). Also IoT connectivity (NB-IoT) for meters and the like |

- In Kenya, many apps are given out free by aid organizations and companies. Farmers often think "free is normal", so charging farmers directly is hard
- As with the failure of the logistics company Twiga Foods (Chapter 5), some companies do not last even after raising money

### Links with Japan

- JICA's **SHEP** (an approach that changes small horticulture farmers to "grow to sell") was born in Kenya and has spread across Africa
- At TICAD 9 in 2025, the Toyota Mobility Foundation, New KCC and JICA held talks on helping small dairy farmers and young people through transport
- JICA's **Project NINJA** connects Kenyan startups with Japanese investors
- There are also companies started in Kenya by Japanese founders (for example [**HAKKI AFRICA**](https://hakki-group.com/), which lends cars to ride-hailing drivers)

## If you were to enter

### Opportunities

- **Collection data for cooperatives and dairies**: record each farmer's volume, quality and temperature, and pay through M-Pesa. Cooperatives and dairies are customers who will pay
- **Pricing by quality**: sensors that detect watered-down milk or milk from cows with mastitis. Dairies want to pay more for clean milk
- **Medium-sized farms** (10–100 cows): collars that detect heat, milk yield and rumination sensors, feed management. Few in number, but able to pay. Japanese cattle technology can be used as is ([Dairy Cow Textbook](https://yusuke1090.github.io/textbooks/?b=cow), Chapter 12)
- **Cooling as a service**: rent coolers instead of selling them, and charge by the volume carried (Savanna Circuit's model)
- **Feed and silage**: monitoring the temperature and moisture of fermenting silage. For contractors who make silage

### Strengths

- The dairy system (cooperatives, factories, artificial insemination, mobile money) is already in place
- You can do business in English. There are engineers and data talent
- There are many investors. Japanese companies and funds are coming too
- With Nairobi as a base, it is easy to expand to Uganda, Tanzania and Rwanda

### Things to watch out for

- **Lots of competition**: there are already many similar app and sensor companies. You also compete with free aid apps
- **It must be cheap to sell**: small farmers can pay very little per month
- **Taxes change often**: each year's budget law changes taxes
- **The personal data law** (2019): companies that handle data register with the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC)
- **Certification of radio equipment**: type approval from the Communications Authority (CA) is needed
- **Each county is different**: subsidies, fees and the people in charge differ by county

### Ways to get in

- Set up a small base in Nairobi and run a 3–6 month trial with one cooperative (in Kiambu, Nyandarua, Meru or elsewhere)
- Connect payments through Safaricom's M-Pesa API (Daraja)
- Get introductions to dairies and cooperatives through JICA (Project NINJA, SHEP) or TICAD connections
- Partner with local startups (Savanna Circuit, DigiCow and others) and take charge of the sensor and hardware side
