## The country at a glance

![Where Tanzania is](images/tanzania-map.jpg)

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Official name | United Republic of Tanzania (mainland Tanganyika + Zanzibar) |
| Capital | Dodoma (the government is moving there). The economic center is Dar es Salaam |
| Population | About 61.7 million (2022 census). About 65 million now |
| Languages | **Swahili** (understood throughout the country), English (law, large companies, universities) |
| Currency | Tanzanian shilling (TZS) |
| President | Samia Suluhu Hassan (2021–; the first woman president) |
| Mobile operators | Vodacom (M-Pesa), Yas (mobile money: Mixx by Yas), Airtel, Halotel |
| Groups it belongs to | Both the EAC (East Africa) and SADC (Southern Africa) |

- The same party (the CCM, the Party of the Revolution) has stayed in power since independence
- In the October 2025 election, President Samia was announced as winning with 97.66%. The main opposition candidates could not run, the internet was shut down, and many deaths were reported from the crackdown on protests. Western countries expressed concern
- The economy grows 5–6% a year. Gold, tourism (safaris, Zanzibar, Kilimanjaro) and agriculture are its pillars
- A large dam (the Julius Nyerere Hydropower Station) has increased the electricity supply. Even so, some rural places have power cuts for hours every day

## Agriculture

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Share of the economy (GDP) | About 26% (2024, government figure; about 23% by World Bank figures) |
| Share of workers | 50–60% |
| Staple crops | Corn, cassava, rice, beans |
| Export crops | Cashew nuts, sesame, coffee, tea, tobacco, cotton, sisal, cloves (Zanzibar), avocados |

- Almost all are small family farms of about 1–2 ha. Many fields rely on rain alone, and irrigation is still limited
- In some years, enough corn and rice is grown to sell to neighboring countries. But when domestic prices rise, the government sometimes suddenly bans exports
- The government set a goal of growing agriculture by 10% a year (Agenda 10/30) and greatly increased the Ministry of Agriculture's budget
- **BBT** (Building a Better Tomorrow, 2022–2030): a government plan to prepare farms with land and irrigation for young people so they can start agricultural businesses
- The southern highlands (Iringa, Mbeya, Njombe) are rainy and cool, and produce avocados, potatoes, tea and dairy cattle

## Dairy

### Numbers

| Item | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| Number of cattle | About 39.2 million (2024/25). Second in Africa after Ethiopia |
| Of which improved dairy cattle | About 1.41 million |
| Milk produced | About 4.0 billion L (2024/25), about 4.2 billion L (2025/26) |
| Milk processed in factories | About 100 million L (2025/26). Only **2–3%** of the total |
| Processing plants | 187. Together they could process more than 1 million L a day, but actually process about 290,000 L a day |

### Cattle and farmers

- Almost all cattle are native **zebu** (cattle with a hump on the back). They are tough against heat and disease, but give only about 1–2 L of milk a day
- Improved dairy cattle (crosses with Holsteins and others) give about 5–10 L a day. They are common in the north around Kilimanjaro and Arusha, in Tanga, and in the southern highlands
- Herding peoples such as the Maasai and Sukuma move with their herds in search of grass and water. The family drinks the milk, and sells what is left
- Much of the milk is carried to town by farmers on bicycles or motorbikes and sold directly to homes and eateries. Buyers boil it before drinking

### The dairy industry

| Company | Location | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tanga Fresh | Tanga (northeast coast) | The country's largest dairy. Collects from about 6,000 small farmers. Can process 120,000 L a day, but runs at only about 15% because not enough milk is collected |
| [ASAS](https://asas.co.tz/ASAS-dairy) | Iringa (southern highlands) | Can process 100,000 L a day |
| [Kilimanjaro Fresh](https://kilimanjarofresh.co.tz/), Milkcom and others | North, Dar es Salaam | |

### Problems

- **No cooling**: even when collection centers have cooling tanks, they are often not running because of power cuts or breakdowns. Milk spoils in a few hours in the heat
- **Milk does not get collected**: the factories have spare capacity, but there is no way to cool and transport the milk, so it does not reach them
- **Dry-season feed**: there is grass in the rainy season, but in the dry season the grass dies and milk falls. Few farmers make silage or hay yet
- **Disease**: East Coast fever (ECF), spread by ticks; diseases spread by tsetse flies; mastitis
- **Quality**: there are problems such as adding water and antibiotic residues, and systems that set prices by quality are still weak

### Government action

- It has raised charges on imported dairy products to increase domestic production. Imports have fallen to about 10 million L a year (2024/25)
- A ten-year dairy plan for 2025–2035 worth about 200 million dollars (breeding, feed, cooling, processing)
- School milk programs (the Tanzania Dairy Board, TDB)

## IoT and agritech companies

| Company or project | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| [**MazaoHub**](https://www.mazaohub.com/) | Solar-powered soil sensors (nutrients, moisture, pH) and AI recommend fertilizer and work. Used by more than 35,000 farmers. Raised 2 million dollars in 2025 |
| **Kilimo Fresh** | Collects vegetables and fruit from small farmers and supplies shops and restaurants in Dar es Salaam |
| [**WASSHA**](https://wassha.com/en/) (a Japanese company) | Rents out IoT solar lanterns through small village shops. Payment by mobile money. JICA has also invested. Based in Dar es Salaam |
| [Tetra Pak](https://www.tetrapak.com/) and the dairy [Jambo](https://jambogroup.co.tz/) | Run five mobile milk collection centers with solar cooling units in Shinyanga Region |
| [Solidaridad](https://www.solidaridadnetwork.org/) and Tanga Fresh | A trial of building hubs near farmers to collect and cool milk |
| [Vodacom](https://vodacom.co.tz/) (M-Pesa) and others | The foundation for payments, loans and insurance for farmers |

- A 2024 survey found that about 17% of Tanzania's roughly 1,000 startups are agritech
- In competitions for young entrepreneurs (such as Heifer International's AYuTe Africa), companies working on cattle feed have won prizes
- But compared with Kenya, little money flows into agritech yet, and many companies are short of funds

## If you were to enter

### Opportunities

- **Make the cooling at collection centers visible**: monitor cooling-tank temperatures, power cuts and the state of generators or solar from a distance, and alert immediately when something stops. This tackles the biggest problem: "the factories have room, but the milk doesn't arrive"
- **Collection records and payments**: record each farmer's milk volume and quality, and pay through M-Pesa and the like. If prices can vary with quality, the problem of adding water also shrinks
- **Dry-season feed**: how to make silage and hay, and managing their storage (temperature and moisture sensors)
- **Water management on irrigated farms**: soil moisture sensors and pump control on BBT farms and on avocado and vegetable farms in the southern highlands

### Strengths

- The second-largest cattle population in Africa. Milk output rises every year, and the government is backing dairy
- Factory capacity is unused, so if collection and cooling improve, it turns into sales right away
- Swahili alone reaches the whole country

### Things to watch out for

- **Politics and the internet**: the internet can be shut down, as after the 2025 election. Build devices to store data and send it when the connection returns
- **Rules can change suddenly**: export bans, taxes, license cancellations and so on can come without warning
- **The personal data law** (2022): foreign companies that handle data also need to register with the Personal Data Protection Commission (PDPC)
- **Certification of radio equipment**: devices that use radio need type approval from the communications regulator (TCRA)
- **Starlink is not yet available** (as of 2026)
- Screens and voice messages shown to farmers should be in **Swahili**

### Ways to get in

- Partner with a dairy (Tanga Fresh, ASAS and others) or a cooperative that runs collection centers, and trial at one site
- Join an aid organization's dairy project (Solidaridad, Heifer International and others) as the technology partner
- Partner with a local startup (MazaoHub and others)
- The Japanese precedent is WASSHA. Renting out devices through village shops and getting paid by mobile money could also work for coolers and sensors
