# 5. Trends today

## What you will learn

- How fast the population and cities are growing
- How smartphones and money (mobile money) have changed daily life
- Startups and the flow of money
- New forms of electricity, internet and transport
- Climate change, politics and relations with other countries

## Many young people, and still growing

- The population is expected to grow from about 1.55 billion (2025) to about 2.5 billion in 2050. Many of the world's children are born in Africa
- The median age is about 19. The working-age population will keep growing, so if there are enough jobs it will be a great force for growth; if not, young people's frustration will rise
- Kenya's 2024 anti-government protests were started by young people known as Gen Z, who gathered through social media (Chapter 7)

## Cities are growing

- Lagos (Nigeria), Kinshasa (Democratic Republic of the Congo) and Cairo are megacities of more than 10 million
- In East Africa, Dar es Salaam (Tanzania) has passed 5 million and Nairobi (Kenya) has passed 4 million. Both are growing fast
- Much of the work in towns is unregistered small business (the informal sector): roadside shops, motorbike taxis, market vendors and so on

## Smartphones and money

- In 2007, **M-Pesa**, which lets you send money by mobile phone, started in Kenya. Even without a bank account, you can send money, pay, save and borrow by text-message menus
- Today, **82%** of Kenyan adults use mobile money (2024). Tanzania's mobile money accounts passed **87 million** (June 2026)
- Because the mobile phone era arrived before landlines and bank branches had spread, this is called "**leapfrogging**"
- Cheap Android smartphones from Chinese makers (Tecno, itel, Infinix) sell in large numbers. But many people still use basic feature phones, so **USSD** (menus you operate by dialing codes like `*150*00#`) and text messages are important
- Business communication happens on WhatsApp. Shop orders and information for farmers also go around on WhatsApp

![An M-Pesa shop (Kenya). Cash is deposited and withdrawn here](images/mpesa.jpg)

## Startups

- African startups raised about 4.1 billion dollars in 2025 (according to Partech), the most since 2022
- By country, Kenya was first with about 1 billion dollars. Kenya, South Africa, Egypt and Nigeria together account for over 70% (the "Big Four")
- The largest sector is finance (fintech), followed by energy and climate, logistics, agriculture (agritech), health and others
- There are failures too. Kenya's food distribution company **Twiga Foods** raised about 185 million dollars, but greatly scaled back its business in 2025, and in 2026 group companies went into administration. Logistics, with its costly trucks and warehouses, is an example of a business that is hard to scale
- Investment from Japan is also increasing. In 2025, at least 12 Japanese companies and funds invested in Kenyan startups. There are also companies started locally by Japanese founders (Chapter 9)

## Electricity, and "electricity as a service"

- About 600 million people in Africa still live without electricity
- What spread to fill this gap is **pay-as-you-go solar** (PAYGo). People pay for solar lanterns and home solar kits a little each day with mobile money. If they do not pay, the device is switched off remotely
- This is **IoT** itself. The device has communication and remote shut-off built in, and manages what has been used and paid. The same mechanism is used for solar pumps, refrigerators, milk coolers and electric motorbikes
- Kenya generates about 90% of its electricity from renewable sources such as geothermal, hydro and wind. Japanese yen loans and Japanese manufacturers are involved in its geothermal power
- On the other hand, Malawi and other countries have frequent power cuts, so devices need battery or solar backup

![A solar-charged lamp (Ethiopia)](images/solar-light.jpg)

## Internet and satellites

- Cities have 4G, and some places have 5G. In rural areas, there are still places that only get 2G, or no signal at all
- **Starlink** (satellite internet) launched in Kenya and Malawi in July 2023. Tanzania applied in 2024, but as of 2026 has still not granted a license
- Governments sometimes shut down the internet or social media during elections and protests (Tanzania in 2025, for example). Devices need to be built to store data even when the network is down

## Transport

- What links towns and villages are **motorbike taxis** (boda boda). They carry people, goods, even milk cans
- Fuel is expensive, so **electric motorbikes** and stations that swap charged batteries are spreading (Spiro and others)
- Ride-hailing apps (Uber, Bolt) are also used in Nairobi and Dar es Salaam

![Boda boda waiting for customers (Bagamoyo, Tanzania)](images/boda.jpg)

![A Nairobi minibus, the "matatu"](images/matatu.jpg)

## Climate change

- From 2020 to 2023, the worst drought in 40 years continued in the "Horn of Africa" (northern Kenya, Ethiopia, Somalia), and many livestock died
- In 2023, Cyclone Freddy struck Malawi, leaving more than 1,000 people dead or missing
- In 2024, a drought caused by El Niño led Malawi, Zambia and Zimbabwe to declare "disasters"
- When rainfall cannot be predicted, rain-fed farms suffer heavy damage. Demand is growing for weather and soil moisture data, irrigation and agricultural insurance

![Livestock killed by drought (Kenya, 2009)](images/drought.jpg)

## Politics

- In 2024–25, protests by young people against tax increases continued in Kenya
- In Tanzania's presidential election in October 2025, incumbent President Samia was announced as winning with 97.66%, but the main opposition candidates could not run, and many deaths were reported from protests and crackdowns. The internet was also shut down
- In Malawi's presidential election in September 2025, former president Mutharika of the opposition won, incumbent President Chakwera conceded, and power changed hands peacefully
- Many countries are struggling to repay foreign debt. Raising taxes to repay debt sometimes creates discontent

## Relations with other countries

- **China**: Africa's largest trading partner. It has lent money to build roads, railways and ports (such as Kenya's Mombasa–Nairobi railway)
- **Japan**: since 1993, it has held "**TICAD**" (the Tokyo International Conference on African Development) to discuss Africa's development. TICAD 9 was held in Yokohama in August 2025. JICA (the Japan International Cooperation Agency) does technical cooperation, sends Japan Overseas Cooperation Volunteers and works with startups (Project NINJA)
- **The United States**: in 2025, the U.S. aid agency USAID was drastically cut, and many agriculture and health projects that depended on aid stopped
- Gulf states, Turkey, India and Russia are also strengthening ties with Africa through investment and the military
- African countries try not to depend on any single country, dealing with many in order to get favorable terms
