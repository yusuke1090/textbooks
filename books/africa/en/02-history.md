## The birthplace of humankind

- Many fossils of human ancestors have been found around the Great Rift Valley
- At **Olduvai Gorge** in Tanzania, the Leakeys found fossils and stone tools of early humans. Nearby at Laetoli, footprints of beings that walked on two legs about 3.66 million years ago remain
- In Ethiopia, the bones of "Lucy", about 3.2 million years old, were found
- If you trace the ancestors of everyone in the world back, you arrive in Africa

## Kingdoms and trade before colonization

- Long before Europeans arrived, Africa had large kingdoms and towns
- **West Africa**: Mansa Musa, king of the Mali Empire, is said to have given away so much gold on his pilgrimage to Mecca in 1324 that the price of gold in Egypt fell. Timbuktu was a town of learning
- **The East African coast**: traded by ship with Arabia, Persia, India and China, sailing on the monsoon winds. Through this trade, Arabic words entered a Bantu language and **Swahili** was born. "Swahili" means "people of the coast" in Arabic
- In the early 15th century, the fleet of Zheng He from Ming China came as far as Malindi in Kenya

## The slave trade

- In the Atlantic slave trade of the 16th–19th centuries, about 12.5 million people were taken to the Americas. Many died on the way
- In East Africa too, people from the interior were brought to Zanzibar and sold to Arabia and the Indian Ocean islands. Zanzibar's slave market was closed in 1873, and a church now stands on the site
- The loss of so many workers left long-lasting scars on the population and economy of those regions

## Division and colonization by Europe

- In the 19th century, explorers such as Livingstone (a British missionary who visited Lake Malawi) entered the interior
- At the Berlin Conference of 1884–85, European countries made rules for dividing up Africa. Borders drawn without regard to peoples or languages became the basis of today's borders
- In the colonies, production of crops (tea, coffee, cotton, tobacco, sisal) and minerals for sending to Europe was pushed, and roads and railways were also built to carry them to the ports

## Independence and after

- In the 1950s and 60s, almost all countries became independent
- After independence, many countries had one-party rule or military coups. During the Cold War, the United States and the Soviet Union each supported governments on their side
- In the 1980s, more countries could not repay their debts, and under the guidance of the International Monetary Fund (IMF) and the World Bank, "structural adjustment" was carried out, cutting subsidies and privatizing state companies
- From the 1990s, more countries held multi-party elections
- From the 2000s, many countries' economies grew strongly thanks to rising resource prices and the spread of mobile phones

## The paths of the three countries

### Tanzania

- From the end of the 19th century it was German East Africa. In 1905–07, the "Maji Maji Rebellion" rose against German rule and forced cotton growing
- After the First World War, it became Tanganyika, governed by Britain
- Tanganyika became independent in 1961 (first president: Nyerere). In 1964 it united with Zanzibar, where a revolution had taken place, to become **Tanzania**
- Nyerere promoted "**Ujamaa**" (Swahili for "familyhood"), an African socialism in which people gathered in villages to farm together, but the economy did not do well. On the other hand, by **making Swahili used throughout the country**, he built a nation where more than 120 ethnic groups came together
- Multiple parties were allowed in 1992. The same party (now the CCM, the Party of the Revolution) has stayed in power since independence

![Nyerere (1965)](images/nyerere.jpg)

### Kenya

- A British protectorate from 1895 and a colony from 1920
- Britain built a railway from Mombasa to Lake Victoria (completed in 1901), bringing many people from India to do it. Nairobi was born as a base for this railway. People of Indian descent still play a large role in Kenyan business
- The cool highlands (the "White Highlands") became European farms, and Africans lost their land
- In 1952–60, the "**Mau Mau uprising**" demanding land and freedom broke out, and Britain declared a state of emergency and cracked down harshly
- Independence in 1963 (first president: Jomo Kenyatta)
- After the 2007–08 election, more than 1,000 people died in fighting between ethnic groups. In 2010 a new constitution divided the country into 47 counties and handed tasks such as agriculture to the counties

![Jomo Kenyatta](images/kenyatta.jpg)

### Malawi

- Livingstone visited Lake Malawi in 1859. The southern city of Blantyre is named after the Scottish town where Livingstone was born
- A British protectorate from 1891 (called "Nyasaland" from 1907). From 1953 to 1963 it was joined in a federation with Rhodesia (today's Zambia and Zimbabwe)
- Independence in 1964 (President Banda). Banda ruled for nearly 30 years under one-party rule as "president for life"
- Multi-party elections began in 1994
- The 2019 presidential election was annulled by the courts because of irregularities, and opposition leader Chakwera won the rerun in 2020. It drew attention as a rare case in Africa
