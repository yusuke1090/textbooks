# 8. Malawi

## The country at a glance

![Where Malawi is](images/malawi-map.jpg)

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Capital | Lilongwe. The commercial center is Blantyre, and the center of the north is Mzuzu |
| Population | About 17.6 million (2018 census). Now over 20 million |
| Area | About 118,000 km² (about a third of Japan). About a fifth is Lake Malawi. A landlocked country |
| Languages | English (official), **Chewa** (Chichewa; the most widely spoken). Tumbuka in the north |
| Currency | Malawian kwacha (MWK) |
| President | Peter Mutharika (won the September 2025 election with 56.8%, becoming president for the second time) |
| Mobile operators | Airtel (Airtel Money), TNM (mobile money: Mpamba) |
| Groups it belongs to | SADC, COMESA |

- Called "**the Warm Heart of Africa**", its people are said to be gentle, and it is said to be safer than its neighbors
- One of the poorest countries in the world. Economic growth in 2025 was about 1.9% (World Bank)
- **Shortage of foreign currency**: foreign money (dollars) is scarce, making it hard to pay for imports. The central bank's foreign reserves covered only 0.4 months of imports (March 2026). The kwacha was devalued by 44% in November 2023; since then the official rate has been about 1,750 kwacha to the dollar, but street money changers trade at higher prices
- Prices rise fast, and fuel sometimes runs short. Power cuts are frequent too
- Support from the International Monetary Fund (IMF) ended in May 2025, and as of October 2026, talks on a new program are continuing
- Starlink has been available since 2023

## Agriculture

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Share of GDP | About a quarter to a third |
| Workers | Mostly in agriculture |
| Share of exports from farm products | About 80% |
| Top export | **Tobacco** (about half to 60% of exports) |
| Other exports | Tea, sugar, soybeans, groundnuts, macadamia, pigeon peas (to India) |
| Staple | Corn (nsima) |

- Most farmers grow mainly corn on fields of less than 1 ha
- There is only one rainy season, November to April, and farming depends on the rain. In the dry season there are also vegetable gardens near rivers and wetlands (called "dimba")
- Tobacco prices have fallen, and the world is moving to reduce tobacco, so growing other crops is a national challenge. In 2020, the country also legalized growing industrial cannabis (hemp)

![A tobacco field](images/tobacco.jpg)

![Husking harvested corn (Kasungu District)](images/maize-dehusk.jpg)

### Weather disasters and food shortages

- 2023: Cyclone Freddy. Six months' worth of rain fell in six days, and floods and landslides left more than 1,000 people dead or missing
- 2024: drought caused by El Niño. 23 of the 28 districts declared "disasters"
- 2025: the corn harvest was about 22% below the five-year average. About 4 million people were expected to be in food crisis from October 2025 to March 2026
- The country needs about 3.5 million tonnes of corn a year

### Fertilizer subsidies

- There is a government subsidy that sells fertilizer and seed cheaply to small farmers ("AIP" from 2020; after the 2025 change of government it went back to its earlier name, "FISP")
- It uses a large part of the national budget, yet fertilizer often arrives late. In December 2025, when fertilizer should have been going on the fields, only 35% of farmers had received it

## Dairy

### Numbers

| Item | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| Dairy cattle | About 55,000 (Malawi Milk Producers Association). There is no cattle registration system, so all figures are estimates |
| Milk per cow per day | 8–15 L |
| Milk drunk per person per year | About 5–8 L. Far below the African average (about 20 L) |
| Share met domestically | About 65% of demand (Food and Agriculture Organization, FAO). The rest is imported as milk powder and the like |
| Price farmers sell at | 725–900 kwacha per L (December 2025). The cost of production is about 890 kwacha per L |

### Producing areas and milk collection

- There are three producing areas: the Shire Highlands in the south (around Blantyre), the center (Lilongwe, Dowa, Mchinji) and the north (Mzuzu)
- **Milk Bulking Groups** (MBGs): since the 1960s, farmers have formed groups that bring their milk to collection centers with cooling tanks and sell it to factories. About half of the milk not drunk at home goes through the centers. The rest is sold directly to townspeople and traders
- Many dairy cows are crossbreds given out by aid projects, and there are also schemes where calves are passed on to other farmers to spread them

![A dairy cow looking out of its shed (a village in central Malawi)](images/malawi-dairy-cow.jpg)

### The dairy industry

- There are about seven processing plants, using only about 40% of their capacity
- In 2019, the major company Dairibord Malawi pulled out after making losses
- In 2023, Malawi Dairy Industries, a big buyer in the center, stopped, and more than 5,000 farmers lost a buyer for their milk. One cause was power cuts that kept the factory from running
- Others include Lilongwe Dairy and Suncrest Creameries (Blantyre). There are also plans to build a milk powder factory
- In March 2025 the government banned imports of raw milk and the like, and in April 2025 set a minimum price for buying from farmers (800 kwacha per L)

### Problems

- **Power cuts**: cooling tanks at collection centers and factories stop
- **Feed costs**: maize bran used as feed gets more expensive in years when corn is expensive
- **Foreign currency**: it is hard to import machines, medicines and semen (for artificial insemination)
- **People**: there are few artificial insemination technicians and vets
- **Price**: the price farmers get sometimes falls below the cost of production

## IoT and agritech companies

Agritech in Malawi is still at a stage with mostly small companies and little information.

| Company or project | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Seedgro** | Collects data from soil sensors buried in fields through IoT and the cloud, and tells farmers by text message, voice and USSD |
| **Mechro Limited** | A Blantyre company. A cheap soil moisture sensor for small farmers (Chameleon) and AI recommendations for crops and work. Placed in the top 10 of the African Telecommunications Union's innovation competition in 2025 |
| [**Nuru Solutions**](https://nuru.solutions/) | Turns agricultural data into decision tools for farmers (started in 2024; the founder came from the insurance company Pula) |
| [**Noble Agriculture Technologies**](https://nobleagritech.com/) | An IoT agriculture company in Lilongwe. Received a climate adaptation award (SEED) in 2019 |
| **Mbora** | Small loans for small farmers |
| **Ulangizi** ([Opportunity International](https://opportunity.org/)) | An AI that answers farming questions on WhatsApp in English or Chewa. It identifies crop diseases from photos. For farmers without smartphones, advisers use it on their behalf |
| **Inyosi Tech Hub** | Nurtures startups and teaches digital skills to young people |

- In 2017, UNICEF and the Government of Malawi set up Africa's first "humanitarian drone testing corridor" in Kasungu District. Trials of carrying samples and medicines by drone were held, and drone engineers are being trained
- Most farmers do not have smartphones, so information is usually delivered by radio, text message, USSD and voice

## Links with Japan

- Japan Overseas Cooperation Volunteers have been sent to this country for a long time and in large numbers
- A program based on Oita Prefecture's "One Village One Product" movement (OVOP) has continued as a national project since 2003
- At TICAD 9 in 2025, a plan to develop Mozambique, Malawi and Zambia together was presented

## If you were to enter

### To be honest

- The market is small, and foreign currency shortages, power cuts and fast-rising prices make it **the hardest of the three countries to enter as an ordinary business**
- Foreign currency to import equipment is hard to get, and revenue received in kwacha tends to lose value
- The easier way in is delivering technology as part of **aid projects** (World Bank, EU, UK, JICA, NGOs)

### Opportunities

- **Monitoring cooling at collection centers, and preparing for power cuts**: keep cooling running with solar and batteries, and alert on temperature and outages. This directly tackles the reason the factory stopped (power cuts)
- **Milk testing**: set prices by quality to raise farmers' income
- **Corn storage**: temperature, humidity and insect sensors for grain in warehouses and homes. Reduce the amount that spoils and is thrown away after harvest
- **Water management for dry-season gardens (dimba)**: cheap soil moisture sensors and pumps
- **A test bed for low-cost design**: something cheap and tough enough to work in Malawi can work in other countries too

### Things to watch out for

- **Foreign currency and money**: importing parts and sending profits abroad are both hard. Set prices in dollars, or get paid from aid budgets
- **Power cuts**: build devices to run on solar and batteries
- **The personal data law** (2024): companies that handle large amounts of data need to register with the Malawi Communications Regulatory Authority (MACRA)
- **Certification of radio equipment**: type approval from MACRA
- **Voice over screens**: few people have smartphones, so use Chewa voice messages, text messages and USSD

### Ways to get in

- Connect with JICA projects and volunteers, and get introduced to milk bulking groups on the ground
- Join an aid organization's dairy or agriculture project as the sensor and visualization partner
- Partner with young local entrepreneurs (Seedgro, Mechro and others) to build a setup that can repair things locally
