# 4. Culture and daily life

## What you will learn

- Ethnic groups, and ties of family and community
- Greetings, time and clothing
- Music, art, sports and film
- Things to keep in mind when meeting for business

## Ethnic groups

| Country | Ethnic groups |
| --- | --- |
| Tanzania | More than 120. Even the largest, the Sukuma, are less than 20% of the population. Swahili holds the country together, and there is little conflict between groups |
| Kenya | More than 40. Kikuyu, Luhya, Kalenjin, Luo, Kamba and others. The Maasai are few in number but famous. Support in elections tends to split clearly along ethnic lines |
| Malawi | Chewa (the largest), Lomwe, Yao, Ngoni, Tumbuka and others |

- It is important not to lump everyone together as "Africans", but to look at countries and ethnic groups. Even within one country, language, food and livelihood (farming, herding or fishing) differ
- The Maasai (southern Kenya, northern Tanzania) are herders who live moving with their cattle. Cattle are wealth, and are also given as a gift at marriage (bridewealth)

## Ties of family and community

- Ties with relatives (the extended family) are strong, and it is normal for someone with a job to pay a cousin's school fees or a relative's hospital bills
- Older people are respected. In Tanzania, you greet an elder with "**Shikamoo**" ("I am at your feet"), and they answer "**Marahaba**"
- **Ubuntu**: a Southern African word for the idea that "a person is a person through other people"
- **Harambee**: a Kenyan word meaning "let's all pull together". Communities collect donations to build schools and hospitals. It is written on Kenya's coat of arms
- **Savings groups**: several people put in money every month and take turns receiving it, or lend it out. They are called "chama" in Kenya, "VICOBA" in Tanzania and "village savings and loan groups" in Malawi, and farmers also use them to buy fertilizer or cattle

## Greetings

- Greetings are long and polite. Before talking business, you ask how the other person and their family are. Going straight to the point feels rude
- People shake hands often. Sometimes they keep holding hands while talking. Use your right hand (with an elder, support your right wrist with your left hand)
- Phrases are collected in Appendix A. Just greeting people in Swahili or Chewa makes them very happy

## Time

- As the word "**pole pole**" (slowly, slowly) suggests, time flows at a relaxed pace. People are often late for appointments. However, meetings between companies in Nairobi increasingly start on time
- **Swahili time**: hours are counted with 6 a.m. as "0 o'clock". "Saa moja" (one o'clock) means 7 a.m. When arranging to meet, check which system is meant

## Clothing

| Name | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Kanga | A pair of brightly colored cotton cloths with a Swahili proverb printed in the middle. Used as a wrap skirt, a shawl or to carry a baby on the back |
| Kitenge (chitenje in Malawi) | Cloth with batik-like patterns, made into clothes |
| Shuka | The red checked cloth of the Maasai |
| Kikoi | A striped cloth men on the coast wrap around the waist |

## Music

| Genre | Country | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bongo Flava | Tanzania | Hip hop and R&B in Swahili. Diamond Platnumz is famous |
| Singeli | Tanzania | Very fast dance music born in Dar es Salaam |
| Taarab | Zanzibar | Songs with string instruments, influenced by Arab and Indian music |
| Benga | Kenya | Dance music with finely bouncing guitar |
| Gengetone | Kenya | Rap by Nairobi youth, sung in slang (Sheng) |
| Afrobeats | Nigeria | Burna Boy, Wizkid and others. Listened to all over the world |
| Amapiano | South Africa | Relaxed dance music with piano and low drums. Spreading across Africa |

- In Malawi, the "Lake of Stars" music festival held on the lakeshore is well known

## Art and crafts

- **Tingatinga**: Tanzanian paintings of animals and more in bright enamel paint. Started by Edward Saidi Tingatinga in the 1960s. Popular in Japan too
- **Makonde carving**: ebony carvings by the Makonde people of southern Tanzania and northern Mozambique

## Sports

- **Football (soccer)** is the most popular. People watch English Premier League matches together on TVs in sheds and eateries in town. In domestic football, the clash between Tanzania's "Simba" and "Young Africans (Yanga)" is hugely popular
- The 2027 Africa Cup of Nations is planned to be held jointly by **Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda**
- **Long-distance running**: Kenya and Ethiopia have set marathon world records many times. Runners from all over the world come to train in the highlands of Kenya's Rift Valley (Iten and elsewhere)

![Kenyan runners in a marathon (2017, Zagreb)](images/marathon.jpg)

## Film and TV

- Nigerian film (Nollywood) is among the largest in the world by number of films made
- Films in local languages are also made, such as Tanzania's "Bongo movies" and Kenya's "Riverwood"
- Recently many people watch YouTube and TikTok on their phones, and local comedians and influencers have great influence

## Tourism

- Safaris: Serengeti and Ngorongoro in Tanzania, Maasai Mara in Kenya. From July to October you can see the "Great Migration", when hundreds of thousands of wildebeest move in search of grass
- Zanzibar's sea and its stone town (Stone Town, a World Heritage Site)
- Climbing Kilimanjaro
- Lake Malawi. Malawi calls itself "**the Warm Heart of Africa**" and is known for the warmth of its people

## Things to keep in mind when meeting for business

- **Relationship first, business second**: you do not sign a contract right after meeting for the first time. Things move forward after meeting several times, having tea and building trust
- **Contact by WhatsApp**: replies come faster on WhatsApp than by email. Voice messages are also used a lot
- **Respect titles**: address people by titles such as "Dr." and "Eng." (engineer). Older men may be respectfully called "Mzee" (elder)
- **Do not criticize in public**: when pointing out a mistake, do it gently and where others cannot hear
- **Courtesy calls on officials**: when doing business in rural areas, visiting county or district officials and the village head is the quickest route
- **Confirm agreements**: "yes" may be politeness in the moment rather than real agreement. Put what you decided in writing
