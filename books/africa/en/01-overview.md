# 1. An overview of Africa

## What you will learn

- Africa's size, number of countries and population
- How the regions are divided, and the major landforms
- Languages and religions
- Groups of countries (AU, EAC, SADC and others)

The first half of this textbook (Chapters 1–5) is about Africa as a whole, and the second half (Chapters 6–9) is about agriculture and dairy in Tanzania, Kenya and Malawi, IoT companies, and how you might enter the market.

## Africa in numbers

| Item | Figure | For comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Area | About 30 million km² | About 80 times Japan. About 20% of the world's land |
| Number of countries | 54 (UN member states) | 55 in the African Union (AU), including Western Sahara |
| Population | About 1.55 billion (2025) | About 1 in 5 people in the world |
| Population in 2050 | About 2.5 billion (UN estimate) | About 1 in 4 people in the world |
| Median age | About 19 | Japan's is about 49 |

- Africa often looks small on maps (because of the Mercator projection), but it is about as large as the United States, China, India and most of Europe put together
- The most populous country is Nigeria (over 200 million), followed by Ethiopia, Egypt and the Democratic Republic of the Congo

## How the regions are divided

| Region | Main countries | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| North Africa | Egypt, Morocco, Algeria | Arabic and Islam. Strong ties with the Mediterranean and the Middle East |
| West Africa | Nigeria, Ghana, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire | Large populations. Cocoa and oil. Many French-speaking countries |
| Central Africa | Democratic Republic of the Congo, Cameroon | Rainforests. Mineral resources (cobalt, copper) |
| East Africa | **Kenya, Tanzania**, Ethiopia, Uganda, Rwanda | Highlands and the Great Rift Valley. Swahili. Tea, coffee, dairy |
| Southern Africa | South Africa, **Malawi**, Zambia, Zimbabwe, Mozambique | Mines and large farms. South Africa is the economic center |

- Everything south of the Sahara is called "sub-Saharan Africa". Statistics often separate it from North Africa
- Malawi is sometimes counted as Southern Africa and sometimes as East Africa

## Major landforms

- **The Sahara Desert**: most of North Africa. The world's largest hot desert
- **The Sahel**: the southern edge of the Sahara. Little rain, with frequent droughts and conflicts
- **The Nile**: one of the longest rivers in the world (about 6,650 km). From Lake Victoria and the Ethiopian Highlands to the Mediterranean
- **The Congo River and rainforest**: the second-largest rainforest after the Amazon
- **The Great Rift Valley**: a long valley formed where the land is being pulled apart east and west. It runs from Ethiopia through Kenya and Tanzania to Lake Malawi. Many fossils of human ancestors have been found there
- **Kilimanjaro** (Tanzania): Africa's highest mountain (5,895 m)
- **Lake Victoria** (Tanzania, Kenya, Uganda): Africa's largest lake
- **Lake Malawi** (Malawi, Mozambique, Tanzania): one of the lakes with the most fish species in the world. About a fifth of Malawi's area

## Climate

- Even in countries near the equator, the highlands are cool. Nairobi in Kenya (about 1,700 m above sea level) is like spring or early summer in Japan all year round
- Seasons are divided by "**rainy season** and **dry season**" rather than "hot and cold"
  - Kenya and northern Tanzania: two rainy seasons (the long rains in March–May, the short rains in October–December)
  - Malawi and southern Tanzania: one rainy season (November–April)
- Most fields depend on rain alone (rain-fed agriculture), so a year with little rain quickly leads to poor harvests and food shortages

## Languages

- Africa is said to have **about 2,000** languages. Dozens of languages in one country is normal
- Colonial-era languages (English, French, Portuguese) are still used as official languages
- Languages used over wide areas

| Language | Where it is used |
| --- | --- |
| Arabic | North Africa |
| **Swahili** | East Africa (an official language in Tanzania and Kenya). Over 100 million speakers |
| Hausa | West Africa (northern Nigeria and elsewhere) |
| Amharic | Ethiopia |
| Yoruba, Igbo | Nigeria |
| Zulu and others | South Africa |
| **Chewa** (Chichewa) | Malawi, eastern Zambia, Mozambique |

## Religion

- North of the Sahara, Islam is the majority; to the south, Christianity
- The East African coast (Zanzibar in Tanzania, Mombasa in Kenya) has long been a Muslim area
- Alongside Christianity and Islam, traditional beliefs that honor ancestors also remain
- The church on Sunday and the mosque on Friday are places where local people gather. Work schedules are planned around them too

## Groups of countries

| Name | Full name | Members (of this book's three countries) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AU | African Union (headquarters in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia) | All |
| AfCFTA | African Continental Free Trade Area. Trading began in 2021 | All |
| EAC | East African Community. It has common tariffs (a common external tariff) | Kenya, Tanzania |
| SADC | Southern African Development Community | Tanzania, Malawi |
| COMESA | Common Market for Eastern and Southern Africa | Kenya, Malawi |

- Tanzania is in both the EAC and SADC, placing it where East and Southern Africa connect
- Tariffs are lower within the same group, which matters when deciding which country to set up a base in
