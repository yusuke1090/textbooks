# 4. Christianity

## What you will learn in this chapter

- An overview of Christianity and the teachings of Jesus
- Its history of spreading from the Roman Empire to the world
- The differences between Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant
- Events, and Christianity in Japan

## Overview

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Believers | About 2.3 billion (2020). The largest religion in the world |
| Main countries | The United States, Brazil, Mexico, the Philippines, Russia, Nigeria, the Democratic Republic of the Congo and others |
| Founder | Jesus (around 4 BCE to around 30 CE). Believers call him "Christ" (the savior) |
| God | The one God. God the Father, Jesus the Son and the Holy Spirit are one God (the Trinity) |
| Scripture | The Bible: the "Old Testament", inherited from Judaism, and the "New Testament", about Jesus and his disciples |
| Place of prayer | The church |
| Leader | Priest (Catholic), priest (Orthodox), pastor or minister (Protestant) |
| Holy places | Jerusalem (where Jesus is said to have died and risen), the Vatican in Rome (the center of the Catholic Church) |

## Origins and history

| When | Event |
| --- | --- |
| Around 4 BCE | Jesus is born in Bethlehem (today's Western calendar was based on Jesus' birth, but it is slightly off) |
| Around 30 CE | Jesus preaches that the kingdom of God is near, heals the sick, and keeps company with the poor and those called sinners. He clashes with Jewish leaders and is executed on a cross by the Roman Empire. His disciples believe "he rose on the third day" and spread the word |
| 1st century | Paul and others spread it to non-Jews too. Repeated persecution within the Roman Empire |
| 313 | The Roman Empire recognizes Christianity (the Edict of Milan) |
| 392 | It becomes the state religion of the Roman Empire |
| 1054 | Split into the Roman Catholic Church in the west and the Orthodox Church in the east |
| 1096–1291 | The Crusades. European countries repeatedly send armies to take the holy city of Jerusalem |
| 1517 | In Germany, Luther criticizes the Church's practices and the Reformation begins. Protestantism is born |
| From the 16th century | Spreads with Spanish and Portuguese ships to Latin America, Africa and Asia. In 1549 Xavier comes to Japan |
| 19th–20th centuries | Missionaries spread it in Africa, Asia and the Pacific islands |
| 2020 | The region with the most believers changes from Europe to sub-Saharan Africa |

## What is believed

- **God's love**: God loves people. People should love God too, and **love your neighbor as yourself** (the two teachings Jesus called most important)
- **Sin and salvation**: all people have sin. Jesus, the Son of God, died on the cross and atoned for people's sins. Those who believe in Jesus are saved and receive eternal life
- **The Resurrection**: Jesus rose again on the third day after his death. The most important event in Christianity
- **The last day**: one day Jesus will come again, judge the world, and the kingdom of God will be completed
- Jesus' teachings are passed on in parables (the Good Samaritan, the Prodigal Son and others) and the "Sermon on the Mount" ("Blessed are the poor in spirit", "Love your enemies")

## Three major branches

| | Catholic | Orthodox | Protestant |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Numbers | About 1.42 billion (Vatican count, 2024) | About 200–300 million | About 800–900 million (total of many denominations) |
| Center | The Pope (the Vatican) | A church for each country (the Russian Orthodox Church, the Greek Orthodox Church and so on). The Patriarch of Constantinople is first in name | No center. Relies on the Bible alone |
| Characteristics | Seven sacraments (baptism, the Eucharist at Mass and so on); honors the Virgin Mary and the saints. Priests do not marry | Values icons (paintings of saints). Old forms of worship. Priests can marry | Values faith and the Bible. Ministers can marry. Very many denominations |
| Where common | Latin America, southern Europe, Poland, the Philippines, Africa | Russia, Eastern Europe, Greece. The Oriental churches such as Ethiopia's are close to it | Northern Europe, northern Germany, the UK, the United States, Africa, South Korea |

- Protestant denominations: Lutheran (Nordic countries, Germany), Reformed and Presbyterian (Switzerland, the Netherlands, Scotland, South Korea), Anglican (the Church of England), Baptist, Methodist, Pentecostal and others
- **Pentecostals and evangelicals** emphasize the power of the Holy Spirit, healing and fervent worship, and are growing quickly in Africa, Latin America and Asia
- New groups that came out of Christianity include the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (Mormons) and Jehovah's Witnesses. Their beliefs differ greatly, so they are treated separately from the mainstream churches

## Rules and events

| Event | When | Contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Worship, Mass | Every Sunday | Sunday is the day Jesus rose. People gather in church to pray, sing hymns and hear the Bible |
| Baptism | On becoming a believer | Purified with water to become a believer. In Catholic and Orthodox churches it is often received as a baby |
| Holy Communion (Mass, the Eucharist) | During worship | Receiving bread and wine to remember Jesus' Last Supper |
| Christmas | December 25 (January 7 for most Orthodox) | Celebrates Jesus' birth |
| Lent | About 40 days before Easter | Praying while cutting back on food and luxuries |
| Easter | March–April (the Sunday after the first full moon after the spring equinox) | Celebrates Jesus' resurrection. The most important festival. Eggs are its symbol |

- There are almost no food rules. However, the Orthodox Church has many fasting periods without meat or dairy, and in some Catholic places it is customary not to eat meat on Fridays

## Christianity around the world

![The Church of St. George in Lalibela (Ethiopia). Carved down into the rock from above in the 12th–13th centuries](images/lalibela.jpg)

![A church service in Nigeria. Christians are increasing in Africa](images/nigeria-church.jpg)

![Simbang Gabi, the dawn Masses before Christmas (the Philippines)](images/simbang-gabi.jpg)

- **Africa**: Ethiopia has had churches since the 4th century. Missionaries spread it in the 19th–20th centuries, and today there are also many African-initiated independent churches and Pentecostals
- **Latin America**: Spain and Portugal brought it in the 16th century, and it became a Catholic region. Now evangelicals are growing rapidly (in Brazil and elsewhere)
- **South Korea**: about 30% of the population is Christian. Seoul has some of the world's largest churches
- **Europe and North America**: fewer people go to church and the unaffiliated are increasing. Disused churches sometimes become homes or shops
- Pope Francis died in April 2025, and in May Leo XIV became the first pope from the United States

## Christianity in Japan

- In 1549, Xavier of the Jesuits came to Kagoshima and spread the faith. Christian daimyo (feudal lords) appeared, and believers are said to have numbered several hundred thousand
- In 1614 the Edo shogunate banned it, and after the Shimabara–Amakusa uprising of 1637–38 the crackdown grew harsher. Believers kept their faith in secret as "hidden Christians"
- In 1865, at Oura Cathedral in Nagasaki, hidden Christians revealed their faith to a priest (the "discovery of believers"). In 1873 the public notice boards of the ban were taken down
- Today believers are about 1% of the population. Even so, Christianity is familiar through mission schools and hospitals, church-style weddings, Christmas and more (Chapter 10)

## Summary

- Believes that Jesus is the savior (Christ), and teaches God's love and love of neighbor. His death on the cross and resurrection are central
- Three major branches: Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant
- The religion with the most believers. Now growing in Africa and Latin America, and declining in Europe and North America
