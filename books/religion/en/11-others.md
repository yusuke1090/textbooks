## Zoroastrianism

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Where it began | Ancient Iran |
| Founder | Zarathustra (Zoroaster). When he lived is unclear: around 1000 BCE, or around the 6th century BCE |
| God | Ahura Mazda, the god of good. He is at war with the evil spirit Angra Mainyu, and people should choose the side of good |
| Believers | Somewhere over a hundred thousand. In Iran, and the people called Parsis in India |

- Fire is regarded as pure and is kept burning without end in temples (which is why it was called "fire worship")
- It was the state religion of Persian dynasties (the Sasanians and others), but became a minority with Islamization from the 7th century. Some moved to India and succeeded in commerce as Parsis (India's Tata group, for example)
- Ideas such as the "Last Judgment", "heaven and hell" and "a savior" are said to have influenced Judaism, Christianity and Islam
- The carmaker Mazda explains that its name comes from its founder's name, Matsuda, and from Ahura Mazda

## The Bahá'í Faith

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Where it began | 19th-century Iran |
| Founder | Bahá'u'lláh (1817–1892) |
| Teachings | God is one, and all religions come from that God. Humanity is one, men and women are equal, science and religion are in harmony, and world peace |
| Center | Haifa, Israel (the world headquarters and gardens) |

- In Iran, where it began, it still faces severe persecution

## African traditional religions

- Sub-Saharan Africa has many traditional religions, one for each people. They have much in common
  - A distant creator god, and nearby spirits of nature and of ancestors
  - Ancestors watch over the family after death, and misfortune comes if they are not honored
  - People who connect spirits and humans (healers, diviners, those who know medicinal herbs) give advice on illness and troubles
- Today many people are Christian or Muslim, but many also keep the ideas and rituals of honoring ancestral spirits ([Africa Textbook](https://yusuke1090.github.io/textbooks/?b=africa), Chapter 1)
- **Vodun**: the religion of Benin and Togo in West Africa. In Benin, January 10 is "Vodun Day", a national holiday

### Religions that crossed to the Americas

- Africans taken by the slave trade of the 16th–19th centuries preserved the worship of their gods by overlaying them on Christian saints

| Religion | Country | Origins |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vodou | Haiti | Benin's Vodun and Catholicism |
| Santería | Cuba | The Yoruba gods of Nigeria and Catholicism |
| Candomblé | Brazil | The gods of the Yoruba and other West Africans |

## Indigenous faiths and shamanism

| Region | Faith |
| --- | --- |
| Australia | The Aboriginal "Dreaming": stories that ancestral spirits made the land, animals and people, and the laws that pass them on. Uluru (Ayers Rock) is a sacred site |
| North America | Indigenous spirits of nature and animals, creation stories and rituals |
| Mongolia, Siberia | Shamans (people who connect spirits and humans). Worship of heaven (Tengri) and nature spirits. People pray for safe travel at Mongolian "ovoo" (cairns of piled stones) |
| Japan (Ainu) | "Kamuy" (gods) are thought to dwell in nature, animals and even tools, and thanksgiving rituals are held |

- Indigenous sacred sites and rituals were banned or destroyed in the colonial era. Today, protecting those rights is considered important. Climbing Uluru was banned in 2019

## Other religions in Asia

| Religion | Country | Contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cao Dai | Vietnam | Founded in 1926. Tries to unite Buddhism, Taoism, Confucianism, Christianity and Islam into one. Its symbol is the "Divine Eye". Its saints include the French writer Victor Hugo |
| Tiandao, Yiguandao | Taiwan and elsewhere | Folk religions combining Confucianism, Buddhism and Taoism |
| Yazidism | Northern Iraq and elsewhere | An ancient, distinctive faith. In 2014 the extremist group IS attacked them; many were killed or abducted |
| The Druze | Lebanon, Syria, Israel | Branched off from Islam. Outsiders cannot become believers |

## Summary

- Zoroastrianism: the struggle of good and evil in ancient Iran. Fire temples. Influenced later religions
- The Bahá'í Faith: all religions come from one God. The oneness of humanity
- African traditional religions: ancestral and nature spirits. In the Americas, they became Vodou, Santería and Candomblé
- Indigenous faiths and shamanism. There are also new religions that combine several religions, such as Cao Dai
