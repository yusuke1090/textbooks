# 7. Buddhism

## Overview

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Believers | About 320 million (2020). The only major religion whose numbers have fallen since 2010 |
| Main countries | Thailand (about 68 million), China (about 53 million), Myanmar, Japan, Sri Lanka, Vietnam, Cambodia and others. 98% of believers are in Asia |
| Countries with a Buddhist majority | Cambodia (97%), Thailand (94%), Myanmar (89%), Bhutan (75%), Sri Lanka (70%), Laos (64%), Mongolia (51%) |
| Founder | The Buddha (Gautama Siddhartha; in Japan, Shaka or O-shaka-sama). "Buddha" means "the awakened one" |
| Scriptures | Sutras (collections of the Buddha's teachings). There are versions in Pali, Sanskrit, classical Chinese and Tibetan |
| Places of prayer | Temples and stupas (towers holding relics such as the Buddha's bones; the origin of Japan's five-story pagodas) |
| Holy places | Lumbini (birthplace, Nepal), Bodh Gaya (place of enlightenment, India), Sarnath (place of the first sermon), Kushinagar (place of death) |

## The life of the Buddha

| When (roughly) | Event |
| --- | --- |
| Around the 5th century BCE (some say the 6th) | Born a prince of the Shakya clan in Lumbini, in today's Nepal |
| Age 29 | Troubled by the suffering of old age, sickness and death, he leaves his family and palace to take up the religious life (renunciation) |
| About 6 years | Practices severe austerities, but does not reach enlightenment. Chooses the "Middle Way", leaning neither toward austerity nor luxury |
| Around age 35 | Meditates under the bodhi tree at Bodh Gaya and attains enlightenment |
| About 45 years after | Gives his first sermon at Sarnath and walks across northern India spreading the teaching. A community of disciples (the sangha) forms |
| Age 80 | Dies at Kushinagar (parinirvana) |

## The Buddha's teachings

| Teaching | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| The Four Noble Truths | ① There is suffering in life (suffering) ② The cause of suffering is craving and attachment (origin) ③ If craving and attachment end, suffering ends (cessation) ④ There is a way to do this (path) |
| The Eightfold Path | Eight right ways to end suffering: right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness and concentration |
| Dependent origination | Everything arises through its relations with other things. Nothing exists by itself alone |
| Impermanence | Everything keeps changing; nothing stays the same |
| Non-self | There is no unchanging "I" |
| Nirvana | The peaceful state of enlightenment in which the fire of craving and attachment has gone out. Escaping rebirth |
| The Five Precepts | Five things followers keep: do not kill living beings, do not steal, no sexual misconduct, do not lie, do not drink alcohol |

- Buddhism inherited the Indian ideas of rebirth and karma, but did not accept caste, and taught that anyone could practice and attain enlightenment
- The "Buddha, the Dharma (teaching) and the Sangha (community of practitioners)" are treasured as the "Three Jewels"

## History of its spread

| When | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 3rd century BCE | King Ashoka of India protects Buddhism and spreads it widely. It reaches Sri Lanka |
| Around the turn of the era | Mahayana Buddhism, aiming to "save all people", arises |
| 1st–3rd centuries | In Gandhara (today's Pakistan and Afghanistan), the first Buddha images are made, influenced by Greek sculpture |
| Around the 1st century | Reaches China along the Silk Road |
| 4th century | Reaches the Korean Peninsula |
| 6th century | Reaches Japan (538 and 552 are both given) |
| From the 7th century | Reaches Tibet, and later Mongolia |
| 5th–13th centuries | Theravada Buddhism spreads from Sri Lanka to Southeast Asia (Myanmar, Thailand, Cambodia, Laos) |
| 12th–13th centuries | In India it almost disappears, absorbed into Hinduism and with temples destroyed by Islamic forces |
| 1956 | India's Ambedkar (who drafted the constitution; of Dalit origin) converts to Buddhism with hundreds of thousands of people, in protest against caste |
| From the 20th century | Zen and meditation (mindfulness) spread in Europe and America |

## Three major branches

| | Theravada | Mahayana | Tibetan Buddhism |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Route of spread | South (southern transmission) | North (northern transmission) | North. Inherits late Indian Buddhism |
| Main countries | Sri Lanka, Thailand, Myanmar, Cambodia, Laos | China, Taiwan, South Korea, Japan, Vietnam | Tibet, Mongolia, Bhutan, parts of Nepal |
| Outlook | Monks who have renounced the world keep strict precepts and practice, aiming for their own enlightenment | The ideal is the "bodhisattva", who seeks enlightenment while saving others. Lay people can be saved too | Adds esoteric Buddhism, using mantras and rituals, to Mahayana teachings |
| Buddhas and bodhisattvas | Mainly the Buddha | Many buddhas and bodhisattvas: Amida, Yakushi (Medicine Buddha), Kannon, Jizo and others | Many buddhas, bodhisattvas and guardian deities. Teachers (lamas) are highly valued |
| Language of the sutras | Pali | Classical Chinese (Japan, China, Korea) | Tibetan |
| Features | Monks go on alms rounds in the morning and do not eat from early afternoon until the next morning. Men may become monks for a time (Thailand, Myanmar) | Many schools: Zen, Pure Land (reciting the nembutsu), devotion to the Lotus Sutra and others | Leaders such as the Dalai Lama are thought to be reborn, and the child is found and made the successor |

## Events

| Event | When (roughly) | Contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vesak (Buddha's Day) | The full moon in May | In Theravada countries, celebrates the Buddha's birth, enlightenment and death together |
| Hana Matsuri (Kanbutsu-e) | April 8 | In Japan and elsewhere, celebrates the Buddha's birth. Sweet tea is poured over a small Buddha statue |
| Jodo-e | December 8 | In Japan, the day the Buddha attained enlightenment |
| Obon | July or August | In Japan, China and elsewhere, ancestral spirits are welcomed and honored |
| Rains retreat (Vassa) | Around July–October | In Theravada countries, monks stay in their temples to practice for the three months of the rainy season |

## Today

- In China, Japan and South Korea, fewer people say they are Buddhist. With no successors for temples and fewer supporting families, Japanese temples are at a turning point too (Chapter 10)
- In Thailand and Myanmar, Buddhism is at the center of the state and society, and monks are greatly respected. On the other hand, some Buddhist monks stirring up hostility toward the Muslim minority has also been a problem
- The 14th Dalai Lama fled from Tibet into exile in India in 1959. He is in conflict with the Chinese government over who will decide his successor
- In Europe and America, meditation, mindfulness and Zen are spreading not only as religion but also as ways to support mental health

## Summary

- Begins with the Buddha's enlightenment. Ending suffering through the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path, aiming for nirvana
- Dependent origination, impermanence, non-self. It taught that anyone could practice and attain enlightenment, and did not accept caste
- Three branches: Theravada (South and Southeast Asia), Mahayana (East Asia) and Tibetan Buddhism
- It almost vanished from India, where it was born, and spread throughout Asia
