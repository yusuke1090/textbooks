# 8. Sikhism and Jainism

## What you will learn in this chapter

- Two more religions born in India
- Sikhism: one God, equality, the turban, free community kitchens
- Jainism: taking "non-harming" of living beings to the utmost

## Sikhism

### Overview

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Believers | About 25–30 million |
| Main places | The state of Punjab in northwest India (about 60% of the state's population). Through migration, also many in Canada, the UK and the United States |
| Founder | Guru Nanak (1469–1539). Ten generations of gurus (teachers) followed |
| God | The one God. Formless; the God of every religion is thought to be the same one God |
| Scripture | The Guru Granth Sahib. The tenth guru declared that after him, not a person but this scripture would be the guru |
| Place of prayer | The gurdwara ("the door of the guru") |
| Holy place | The Golden Temple in Amritsar (Harmandir Sahib) |

- Sikhs are only about 2% of India's population, but they stand out in the military, transport, farming and emigration. The image in Japan that "Indians wear turbans" comes from the appearance of Sikhs

![The Golden Temple and pilgrims bathing in the pool (Amritsar, India). The central shrine of Sikhism](images/golden-temple.jpg)

### History

| When | Event |
| --- | --- |
| Around 1499 | Nanak realizes "there is no Hindu and no Muslim" and begins to teach |
| 16th–17th centuries | Successive gurus compile the scripture and build the Golden Temple. In conflict with the Mughal Empire, two gurus are executed |
| 1699 | The tenth guru, Gobind Singh, creates the community of believers, the "Khalsa", and sets the Five Ks (below) |
| 1799–1849 | A Sikh kingdom in Punjab |
| 1947 | With the partition of India and Pakistan, many Sikhs on the Pakistani side moved to India |
| 1984 | The Indian army storms the Golden Temple, where armed militants seeking their own country had barricaded themselves. Afterwards, Prime Minister Indira Gandhi was assassinated by her Sikh bodyguards, and Sikhs were attacked in many places |

### Teachings and rules

- Sikhs value keeping the one God in mind, working honestly and sharing what they earn
- **Caste is rejected, and men and women are equal**. The ideas of rebirth and karma are shared with Hinduism
- Baptized believers wear the "Five Ks"

| The Five Ks | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| Kesh | Hair and beard are not cut. The hair is wrapped in a turban |
| Kanga | A wooden comb |
| Kara | A steel bracelet |
| Kachera | Cotton undergarments |
| Kirpan | A small sword (a sign of protecting the weak) |

- Men add "Singh" (lion) to their names, and women "Kaur" (princess)
- Tobacco, alcohol and meat slaughtered by ritual methods (halal and so on) are avoided

### Langar (free community kitchens)

- Gurdwaras have a kitchen (langar) where anyone can eat for free, regardless of religion, status or nationality. Everyone sits in rows on the floor and eats the same food, expressing equality
- The cooking and cleaning are done by believers as volunteers. The Golden Temple's langar is said to feed 50,000–100,000 people a day

## Jainism

### Overview

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Believers | About 4.5 million (India's 2011 census). Almost all in India |
| Founder | Vardhamana (Mahavira, "the great hero"). A contemporary of the Buddha (6th–5th century BCE). There are said to have been 23 earlier founders (tirthankaras) before him |
| Goal | Purifying the soul of the stain of karma and escaping rebirth |
| Places of prayer | Temples. Many are beautiful temples of white marble |
| Sects | The Svetambara, who wear white clothes, and the Digambara, whose monks wear no clothes |

### Teachings and rules

| Rule | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| Non-harming (ahimsa) | Do not harm any living being. The most important teaching |
| Food | No meat, fish or eggs. Many also avoid root vegetables such as potatoes, onions and carrots, so as not to harm small creatures in the soil. Some do not eat after sunset |
| Monks | Some cover their mouths with cloth so as not to breathe in small insects, and sweep the path with a broom before walking |
| Five vows | Do not kill, do not lie, do not steal, sexual restraint, do not own too much |
| Many-sidedness | One thing can be seen in many ways, and no single view can be said to be the only right one |

- Because they avoided work that kills living beings (including farming and fishing), many went into commerce, finance and jewelry, and have played a large role in India's economy
- Gandhi's idea of nonviolence is also said to have been influenced by Jainism

![Inside the Jain temple at Ranakpur (Rajasthan, India). It has more than 1,400 marble pillars](images/ranakpur.jpg)

## Summary

- Sikhism: founded by Nanak in the 15th century. One God, rejection of caste, and equality. The turban and the Five Ks, and the langar where anyone can eat
- Jainism: Mahavira, a contemporary of the Buddha. Takes non-harming to the utmost; many do not even eat root vegetables
- Both inherited the Indian ideas of rebirth and karma while making their own paths
