# 6. Hinduism

## Overview

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Believers | About 1.2 billion (2020). The third-largest religion in the world |
| Main countries | India (about 95% of the world's believers), Nepal (81% of the population), Bangladesh, Mauritius (48%). Bali in Indonesia. Through migration, also the United States, the UK and the Middle East |
| Founder | None. It took shape over more than 3,000 years within Indian life |
| Gods | Many gods. Brahma (creates), Vishnu (preserves) and Shiva (destroys and renews) are the three great gods. People often choose a favorite god to worship |
| Scriptures | The Vedas (the oldest), the Upanishads (philosophy), the epics "Mahabharata" (containing the "Bhagavad Gita") and "Ramayana" |
| Places of prayer | Temples (mandir) and home altars |
| Holy places | The Ganges (Ganga) River, and Varanasi on its bank, among others |

- "Hindu" originally meant "people beyond the Indus River" in Persian. Believers themselves sometimes call it "Sanatana Dharma" (the eternal law)

## Main gods

| God | Role and form |
| --- | --- |
| Vishnu | Preserves the world. Appears in different forms in times of trouble (avatars). Rama and Krishna are avatars |
| Shiva | Destroys and renews the world. God of dance and of ascetics. Sometimes represented by a linga (a stone pillar) |
| Brahma | Creates the world. However, very few temples are dedicated mainly to Brahma |
| Ganesha | The elephant-headed god. God of beginnings and business. Worshipped when starting something |
| Lakshmi | Goddess of wealth and good fortune (Vishnu's wife) |
| Saraswati | Goddess of learning and the arts |
| Durga, Kali | Powerful goddesses who fight evil |
| Hanuman | The monkey god. He helped Rama |

## Origins and history

| When | Event |
| --- | --- |
| Around 2600–1900 BCE | The Indus civilization. It is thought to have had elements that link to later Hinduism |
| Around 1500 BCE | The Aryans enter northwest India, and a religion forms around hymns to the gods, the "Vedas", and priests (Brahmins) who perform fire rituals (Brahmanism) |
| Around 800–500 BCE | The philosophy of the Upanishads. The ideas of rebirth and liberation take shape. Around the 5th century BCE, Buddhism and Jainism are born |
| Several centuries around the turn of the era | Epics and law books (the "Laws of Manu") appear, and combine with the worship of folk gods to form today's Hinduism |
| 4th–6th centuries | The Gupta dynasty. Worship at temples and of divine images spreads |
| From the 6th century | The "bhakti" movement, preaching wholehearted love of God, spreads from South India. It also reaches Southeast Asia (Angkor Wat in Cambodia was originally a temple of Vishnu) |
| 13th–19th centuries | Islamic dynasties (the Mughal Empire and others) rule North India |
| 19th century | Reform movements arise under British colonial rule |
| 1947 | India and Pakistan become independent, divided by religion. Many people migrated, and many died in the violence |

## What is believed

| Idea | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| Rebirth (samsara) | When a living being dies, it is reborn as something else. This goes on and on |
| Karma | Good deeds bring good results and bad deeds bad results, deciding the next birth |
| Liberation (moksha) | Escaping the cycle of rebirth is the final goal |
| Brahman and Atman | The foundation of the universe (Brahman) and one's true self (Atman) are originally the same. Realizing this brings liberation |
| Dharma | The duties a person should keep; the right way of living |
| Paths to liberation | The path of knowledge (philosophy, meditation), the path of action (doing one's duty without seeking reward), the path of devotion (loving God wholeheartedly). Yoga is also one method of practice |

## Caste

- In Hindu society, a system in which birth determines status and occupation has continued
- **Varna**: four groups: Brahmins (priests), Kshatriyas (kings, warriors), Vaishyas (merchants and others) and Shudras (other workers). Outside them were people who suffered terrible discrimination (now called "Dalits")
- **Jati**: in actual life, thousands of groups by occupation and region (jati) have decided marriage partners and social relations
- India's constitution (1950) forbids discrimination by caste, and reserves places for Dalits and others in university admissions and public jobs. Even so, its influence remains in marriage and village life
- Many people who opposed caste converted to Buddhism, Christianity or Sikhism (Ambedkar in Chapter 7)

## Rules and daily life

| Rule | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| Cows | Cows are sacred animals. Almost everyone avoids beef. In many states, killing cows is forbidden by law |
| Vegetarianism | Many are vegetarian, not eating meat, fish or eggs (especially Brahmins, and in South and West India). Those who eat meat often avoid beef |
| Puja | Worship by offering flowers, lamps, incense and food to an image of a god. Done daily at home altars too |
| Marks | A mark on the forehead (tilaka). The red dot married women wear on the forehead (bindi), and red powder in the hair parting |
| Life rituals | Naming, the ceremony of receiving the sacred thread, weddings (walking around a fire seven times) |
| Funerals | Cremation, with the ashes scattered in a river. The Ganges is especially revered |

## Festivals

| Festival | When (roughly) | Contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diwali (festival of lights) | October–November | Lamps and candles are lit to welcome Lakshmi. Fireworks, sweets, new clothes. India's biggest festival |
| Holi (festival of colors) | February–March | Celebrates spring; people throw colored powder and water at each other |
| Navaratri, Durga Puja | September–October | A nine-night festival honoring the goddess. In Kolkata in East India, large images of Durga are made |
| Ganesh Chaturthi | August–September | Ganesha statues are honored and finally immersed in rivers or the sea |
| Kumbh Mela | Once every 12 years and so on | Tens of millions gather to bathe where certain rivers meet. The largest gathering of people in the world |

## Today

- In India, "Hindu nationalism", which seeks to put Hindu ideas at the center of the nation, has grown stronger, and the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) holds power. Relations with Muslims and other minorities are debated at home and abroad
- In 2006–08, Nepal changed from a "Hindu kingdom" into a secular state, and also abolished the monarchy
- Yoga and meditation have spread around the world, apart from religion

## Links with Japan

Some Indian gods came to Japan with Buddhism by way of China, and became gods protecting Buddhism (the tenbu).

| Name in Japan | Original Indian god |
| --- | --- |
| Bonten | Brahma |
| Taishakuten | Indra (god of thunder) |
| Benzaiten (Benten) | Saraswati |
| Kichijoten | Lakshmi |
| Daikokuten | Mahakala (another form of Shiva) |
| Enma | Yama (king of the dead) |
| Kangiten (Shoten) | Ganesha |

## Summary

- A religion with no founder, formed in India over more than 3,000 years. It has many gods
- Rebirth, karma and liberation. Living by fulfilling one's dharma (duty)
- Cows are sacred and many are vegetarian. Diwali and Holi are major festivals
- 95% of believers are in India. Japan's Benten and Daikokuten were originally Indian gods
