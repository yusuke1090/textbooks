# 9. Confucianism and Taoism (China, Korea, Taiwan)

## What you will learn in this chapter

- The ideas of Confucianism and Taoism, born in China
- How religion is part of daily life in China, South Korea and Taiwan
- The relationship between today's China and religion

## Overview of East Asian religion

- In East Asia, rather than believing in just one religion, it is normal to **use Confucianism, Taoism, Buddhism, ancestral rites and folk gods in different situations**
- Many people answer "no" when asked "do you believe in a religion?", yet often visit graves, burn incense, carry amulets and consult fortune-tellers. This is because the word "religion" in Chinese and Japanese is easily taken to mean joining a religious organization
- In a 2023 Pew survey, about half or more of adults in Taiwan, Japan and Vietnam said that "spirits dwell in mountains, rivers and trees"

## Confucianism

### Overview

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Founder | Confucius (551–479 BCE). From the state of Lu (today's Shandong Province) in China's Spring and Autumn period |
| Books | The "Analects" (sayings of Confucius and his disciples). The Four Books (Analects, Mencius, Great Learning, Doctrine of the Mean) and the Five Classics |
| What is valued | The "five constants": benevolence (ren), righteousness (yi), propriety (li, etiquette and manners), wisdom (zhi) and trustworthiness (xin). "Filial piety" (xiao), caring for one's parents |
| Places of worship | Confucian temples. At home, ancestral tablets |

- Confucianism teaches the right way to live as a person and the order of family, society and state, rather than worshipping gods. Opinions differ on whether it is a religion or a moral and political philosophy
- Even so, rituals honoring Confucius and rites honoring ancestors have long been valued

![The Hall of Great Achievement at the Temple of Confucius (Qufu, Shandong, China), in Confucius' hometown](images/confucius-temple.jpg)

### History

| When | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 6th–5th centuries BCE | Confucius teaches that a disordered world should be governed by benevolence and propriety |
| 4th–3rd centuries BCE | Mencius (human nature is good) and Xunzi (human nature is bad) develop the teaching |
| 2nd century BCE | It becomes the state learning under the Han dynasty |
| 7th century–1905 | Confucian books are tested in the examinations for selecting officials (the imperial examinations) |
| 12th century | Zhu Xi systematizes Neo-Confucianism. It later becomes the official learning of Korea's Joseon dynasty and Japan's Edo shogunate |
| 20th century | Fiercely attacked as old thinking during China's Cultural Revolution (1966–76). Today the Chinese government is reappraising it as traditional culture |

### Confucianism in daily life

- Respecting elders, parents and teachers. Manners such as polite speech and the order of pouring drinks
- Honoring ancestors. In South Korea, many families hold ancestral rites (jesa), offering food to ancestors at New Year and the autumn harvest festival (Chuseok)
- Valuing learning and examinations (said to be linked to the intensity of exam preparation in East Asia)

## Taoism

### Overview

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Founding figures | Laozi ("Tao Te Ching") and Zhuangzi. Little is known about who Laozi really was |
| What is valued | The "Tao" (the Way): the great flow of the universe and nature. People should live naturally without forcing things (wu wei) |
| Goal | Long life and immortality (the immortals). Nourishing body and mind |
| Gods | Very many: the Jade Emperor, Guandi (Guan Yu of the Three Kingdoms; a god of business), Mazu (goddess of the sea), the god of wealth, earth gods, the kitchen god and others |
| Places of worship | Taoist temples (daoguan), shrines |

- In the 2nd century, Zhang Ling founded a religious group called the "Way of the Five Pecks of Rice" (the Way of the Celestial Masters), and this is said to be the beginning of Taoism as a religion
- The ideas of yin and yang, feng shui, tai chi, the thinking of traditional Chinese medicine, paper charms and fortune-telling are connected with Taoism
- It is often said that if Confucianism teaches "the right way to live in society", Taoism teaches "a free way of living in nature, and caring for the body"

## Chinese Buddhism and folk religion

- Buddhism arrived along the Silk Road around the 1st century, mixed with Chinese thought, and gave rise to Zen (Chan) and Pure Land teachings (Chapter 7)
- The bodhisattva Guanyin (Kannon) is especially popular
- At the Spring Festival (Lunar New Year), Qingming (grave visits), the Mid-Autumn Festival and other times, people burn incense and burn paper money to send to their ancestors

## Today's China and religion

- The Chinese Communist Party is atheist, and party members are not supposed to believe in religion
- The state recognizes five religions (Buddhism, Taoism, Islam, Catholicism and Protestantism), and has each operate through state-supervised organizations. Unregistered churches and others may be cracked down on
- In Pew's 2020 estimate, about 10% said they had a religion, the lowest share in the world. Even so, ancestral rites and visits to folk gods are widely practiced
- The government's crackdown on the Muslims (Uyghurs) of the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region and on Tibetan Buddhism has become an international issue

## South Korea

- About half have no religion, about 30% are Christian (mostly Protestant), and just under 20% are Buddhist
- Christianity spread rapidly in the 20th century. There are many large churches, with great influence on society and politics
- Confucian manners and ancestral rites remain strong regardless of religion
- Shamanic rituals (gut) by shamans called mudang also continue

## Taiwan

- A faith mixing folk religion, Taoism and Buddhism is the majority, and there are shrines all over town
- Worship of the sea goddess **Mazu** is especially lively. Every year, millions take part in a pilgrimage that carries Mazu's statue on foot for hundreds of kilometers

## Summary

- In East Asia, it is normal to follow Confucianism, Taoism, Buddhism and ancestral rites together
- Confucianism: Confucius. Benevolence, propriety, filial piety. The order of family and society. Ancestral rites
- Taoism: Laozi. The Tao and acting without forcing. Long life, yin and yang, feng shui
- In China the state manages religion. South Korea has many Christians, and in Taiwan folk religion and Taoism flourish
