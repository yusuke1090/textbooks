# 3. Judaism

## What you will learn in this chapter

- An overview of Judaism and its long history
- The "covenant" with the one God, and the law (Torah)
- The Sabbath, food rules and festivals
- Links with Christianity and Islam

## Overview

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Believers | About 15 million (2020). 0.2% of the world's population |
| Main countries | Israel (about half) and the United States. 85% in these two countries |
| God | The one God. His name (YHWH) is not spoken; he is called "the Lord (Adonai)" or "the Name (HaShem)" |
| Scripture | The Hebrew Bible (Tanakh; the same contents as the Christian "Old Testament"). Most important are the first five books, the "Torah" (the law). The "Talmud" collects its interpretation |
| Place of prayer | The synagogue |
| Leader | The rabbi (a teacher; someone learned in the law) |
| Holy city | Jerusalem |
| Language | Prayers are in Hebrew |

![The Western Wall and plaza at night (Jerusalem). The golden dome behind is the Islamic Dome of the Rock](images/western-wall.jpg)

- Judaism is the religion from which Christianity and Islam came. All three believe in the same God and regard Abraham as the ancestor of their faith
- "Jewish" is not only a religion but also a people, a culture and family ties. Many people consider themselves Jewish even if they do not practice the religion

## Origins and history

| When | Event |
| --- | --- |
| Around 2000 BCE (tradition) | Abraham is said to have heard God's voice and moved to Canaan (today's Israel and Palestine) |
| Around the 13th century BCE (tradition) | Moses leads the people who were slaves in Egypt out (the Exodus). On Mount Sinai he receives the "Ten Commandments" from God |
| Around 1000 BCE | The kingdom of King David and King Solomon. A temple is built in Jerusalem |
| 586 BCE | The Neo-Babylonian Empire destroys the temple and takes the people to Babylon (the Babylonian exile) |
| 515 BCE | People who returned to Jerusalem rebuild the temple (the Second Temple) |
| 70 CE | The Roman Empire destroys the Second Temple. What remains is its western wall (today's "Western Wall") |
| From the 2nd century | Many Jews live scattered across the Middle East, Europe and North Africa (the diaspora). Instead of the temple, synagogues and rabbis become the center of faith |
| Middle Ages to modern times | Repeated discrimination and expulsion in Europe (such as the expulsion from Spain in 1492) |
| Late 19th century | "Zionism", the movement to build their own country in their ancestral land, arises |
| 1933–45 | The Holocaust by Nazi Germany. About 6 million Jews were killed |
| 1948 | Israel is founded. Conflict with the Palestinian people who already lived there continues today |

## What is believed

- **The one God**: God created the world and moves history. God has no form, so no images of God are made
- **The covenant**: God chose the descendants of Abraham (the people of Israel) and made a promise with them. The people keep God's teachings
- **The law (Torah)**: keeping the rules God gave in daily life is faith itself. By tradition, there are 613 commandments
- **Valuing deeds**: how you live counts for more than what you believe
- **The Messiah (savior)**: Jews wait for a savior who will one day come and bring peace to the world. Christianity holds that "Jesus is the Messiah", but Judaism does not

### The Ten Commandments (main points)

1. You shall have no other gods besides me
2. You shall not make images and worship them
3. You shall not take God's name in vain
4. Keep the Sabbath
5. Honor your father and mother
6. You shall not kill
7. You shall not commit adultery
8. You shall not steal
9. You shall not bear false witness
10. You shall not covet your neighbor's things

## Rules

| Rule | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| The Sabbath (Shabbat) | From Friday sunset to Saturday sunset. No work; families eat together and pray. Some keep it strictly, not lighting fires or switching on electricity |
| Kosher (what may be eaten) | Animals with split hooves that chew the cud (cattle, sheep) and fish with fins and scales are allowed. Pork, shrimp, crab, shellfish and eels are not. Meat and dairy are not eaten together. Only meat slaughtered in the prescribed way |
| Circumcision | Boys are circumcised on the 8th day after birth |
| Coming of age | Boys have a bar mitzvah at 13, girls a bat mitzvah at around 12. They read from the Torah in public |
| Kippah | A small cap men wear on their heads. A sign of reverence for God |

![A Shabbat table. Bread under a cloth (challah) and candlesticks](images/shabbat.jpg)

## Festivals

| Festival | When (roughly) | Contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rosh Hashanah (New Year) | September–October | The New Year of the Jewish calendar. A ram's horn (shofar) is blown |
| Yom Kippur (Day of Atonement) | 10 days after New Year | A day of fasting and repentance for sins. The most important day |
| Pesach (Passover) | March–April | Remembers the Exodus. For a week, unleavened bread (matzah) is eaten |
| Hanukkah | November–December | Celebrates the recovery of the temple. For 8 days, one more candle is lit each night |

## Denominations

| Denomination | Characteristics |
| --- | --- |
| Orthodox | Keeps the law strictly, as of old. Includes the "ultra-Orthodox" (Haredim) in black clothes and beards |
| Conservative | Values tradition while adapting little by little to the times |
| Reform | Interprets the law for the times. There are women rabbis. Common in the United States |
| Secular Jews | Keep few religious rules, but value the culture and festivals |

- Jews are also divided by the regions where their ancestors lived: Ashkenazim (German and Eastern European) and Sephardim (Spanish, Middle Eastern and North African)

![Inside a synagogue (Liverpool, UK)](images/synagogue.jpg)

## Today

- In Israel, Jewish rules are part of national life too (in some places public transport stops on the Sabbath, and marriage follows religious rules)
- The ultra-Orthodox have many children, and their share within Israel is growing
- Jerusalem is a city where the holy places of Judaism, Christianity and Islam come together. On the "Temple Mount", where the Jewish temple once stood, now stand the Islamic Dome of the Rock and al-Aqsa Mosque (Chapter 5)

## Links with Japan

- In 1940, Chiune Sugihara at the Japanese consulate in Lithuania issued visas to Jews fleeing the Nazis, saving many lives
- Few Jews live in Japan; there are synagogues in Tokyo and Kobe

## Summary

- Judaism values the covenant with the one God, and living by the law is the center of faith
- The Sabbath (Friday night to Saturday), kosher food, and festivals such as Passover and Hanukkah
- It is the root of Christianity and Islam. Jerusalem is the holy city of all three religions
