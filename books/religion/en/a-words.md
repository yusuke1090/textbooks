# Appendix A. Glossary

## General

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Monotheism | Religions that hold there is only one God (Judaism, Christianity, Islam, Sikhism) |
| Polytheism | Religions that believe in many gods (Hinduism, Shinto and others) |
| Founded religion | A religion whose founder is clearly known |
| Ethnic religion | A religion with no founder, formed within a people's way of life |
| Scripture | A book collecting a religion's important teachings |
| Revelation | God conveying his will or words to people |
| Prophet | A person entrusted with God's words who passes them on to people (not the same as a fortune-teller) |
| Conversion | Changing one's religion |
| Secularization | The weakening of religion's influence in society and daily life |
| Separation of religion and state | Keeping the state and religion apart |
| No religion (unaffiliated) | People who say they belong to no religion. Some still believe in gods or spirits |

## Abrahamic religions

| Term | Religion | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Torah | Judaism | The law. The first five books of the Bible |
| Talmud | Judaism | A book collecting interpretations of and debates on the law |
| Synagogue | Judaism | A meeting house. A place of prayer and study |
| Rabbi | Judaism | A teacher learned in the law |
| Shabbat | Judaism | The Sabbath (Friday sunset to Saturday sunset) |
| Kosher | Judaism | Food that is permitted to be eaten |
| Trinity | Christianity | The belief that God the Father, Jesus the Son and the Holy Spirit are one God |
| Old Testament, New Testament | Christianity | The part inherited from Judaism, and the part about Jesus and his disciples |
| Sacrament | Christianity | Rituals such as baptism and the Eucharist through which God's grace is received (seven in the Catholic Church) |
| Mass | Christianity | Catholic worship |
| Icon | Christianity | A painting of a saint, treasured in the Orthodox Church |
| Reformation | Christianity | The movement to reform the Church begun by Luther and others in the 16th century. Protestantism was born from it |
| Evangelicals | Christianity | A Protestant movement emphasizing the Bible and personal conversion |
| Allah | Islam | "God" in Arabic |
| Muslim | Islam | A believer in Islam |
| Qur'an (Koran) | Islam | The scripture |
| Hadith | Islam | Records of Muhammad's words and deeds |
| Six articles of faith and five pillars | Islam | Six things believed and five practices |
| Ramadan | Islam | The month of fasting |
| Hajj | Islam | The pilgrimage to Mecca |
| Halal, haram | Islam | Permitted, forbidden |
| Sharia | Islam | Islamic law |
| Sunni, Shia | Islam | The two major branches |
| Mosque | Islam | A place of worship |

## Indian religions

| Term | Religion | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Samsara | Indian religions | The repeating cycle of rebirth |
| Karma | Indian religions | The idea that actions determine later results |
| Moksha | Hinduism and others | Liberation from the cycle of rebirth |
| Dharma | Hinduism, Buddhism | In Hinduism, the duties one should keep. In Buddhism, the Buddha's teaching |
| Vedas | Hinduism | The oldest scriptures |
| Puja | Hinduism | Worship by making offerings to an image of a god |
| Caste | Hinduism | The system of status by birth (varna and jati) |
| Four Noble Truths, Eightfold Path | Buddhism | The four truths about suffering, its cause and how to end it, and the eight right ways |
| Nirvana | Buddhism | The state of enlightenment in which craving and attachment have vanished |
| Theravada Buddhism | Buddhism | The Buddhism of South and Southeast Asia. Emphasizes the practice of monks |
| Mahayana Buddhism | Buddhism | The Buddhism of East Asia. Its ideal is the bodhisattva, who saves all people |
| Bodhisattva | Buddhism | One who seeks enlightenment while saving others |
| Stupa | Buddhism | A tower holding relics such as the Buddha's bones |
| Guru | Sikhism and others | A teacher. In Sikhism, the ten gurus and the scripture |
| Gurdwara | Sikhism | A place of worship |
| Langar | Sikhism | A kitchen where anyone can eat for free |
| Ahimsa | Jainism and others | Non-harming. Not hurting living beings |

## East Asia and Japan

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Five constants | Confucianism's benevolence, righteousness, propriety, wisdom and trustworthiness |
| Filial piety | Caring for one's parents and ancestors |
| Tao (the Way) | In Taoism, the great flow of the universe and nature |
| Wu wei | Living naturally, without forcing things |
| Eight million kami | Shinto's many gods |
| Ujiko | People who live in a shrine's area and support the shrine |
| Danka | Families who entrust funerals and memorial services to a temple and support it |
| Shinbutsu-shugo | Worshipping kami and buddhas together |
| Honji suijaku | The idea that Japan's kami are buddhas who appeared in different forms |
| Separation of Shinto and Buddhism | The Meiji government's division of shrines from temples in 1868 |
| State Shinto | Shinto managed by the state as national ritual from the Meiji era until 1945 |
| New religions | Religious groups that arose from the end of the Edo period onward |
