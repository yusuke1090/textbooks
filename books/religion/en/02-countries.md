# 2. Countries and religion

Unless stated otherwise, figures are Pew's 2020 estimates. Where a census year is given, it is that country's own survey. All are rough guides, rounded and marked "about".

## Countries with the most believers of each religion

| Religion | Countries with the most believers | Countries where it is the majority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Christianity | The United States, Brazil, Mexico, the Philippines, Russia, Nigeria, the Democratic Republic of the Congo and others | 120 countries and territories. Common in Europe, the Americas, sub-Saharan Africa and Oceania |
| Islam | Indonesia (about 240 million), Pakistan (about 230 million), India (about 210 million), Bangladesh (about 150 million) | The Middle East and North Africa (94% of the population), Central Asia, parts of South and Southeast Asia, parts of West Africa |
| Hinduism | India (about 95% of the world's Hindus) | India (79%), Nepal (81%). Also the largest in Mauritius (48%) |
| Buddhism | Thailand (about 68 million), China (about 53 million) | Cambodia (97%), Thailand (94%), Myanmar (89%), Bhutan (75%), Sri Lanka (70%), Laos (64%), Mongolia (51%) |
| Judaism | Israel, the United States (85% in these two countries) | Israel (about three-quarters) |
| No religion | China (about 1.3 billion), the United States (about 100 million), Japan (about 73 million) | China, North Korea, Japan, Vietnam, the Czech Republic, Hong Kong, Macau, the Netherlands, Uruguay, New Zealand |

## East Asia

| Country | Largest religions | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Japan | No religion (about half; 40–60% depending on the survey), Buddhism, Shinto | Many people visit both shrines and temples (Chapter 10) |
| China | No religion (about 90%) | Buddhism (4%), Christianity, Islam (Hui and Uyghur peoples), Taoism. Many people worship ancestors and folk gods. Religion is managed by the state (Chapter 9) |

| Taiwan | Folk religion, Taoism, Buddhism | The only place in the world where "other religions" are the majority (Chapter 9) |
| Mongolia | Buddhism (Tibetan Buddhism, about half), no religion | Shamanism (nature spirits) also remains |
| North Korea | No religion | The state strictly restricts religion |

## The Middle East and North Africa

A region where 94% of the population is Muslim.

| Country | Largest religions | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Saudi Arabia | Islam (mostly Sunni) | Home to the holy cities of Mecca and Medina. Many foreign workers follow other religions |
| Iran | Shia Islam | Shia Islam is the state religion. A religious leader is the country's Supreme Leader |
| Iraq | Islam (Shia majority, Sunni) | Christian and Yazidi minorities |
| Turkey | Islam (Sunni) | The constitution separates religion and state (secularism). In Istanbul is Hagia Sophia, once a church of the Eastern Roman Empire |
| Egypt | Islam (about 90%) | Christianity (the Coptic Orthodox Church) about 10% |
| Israel | Judaism (about three-quarters) | Arab Muslims about 20%, Christians, Druze |
| Lebanon | Islam (Sunni and Shia) and Christianity | A system that divides the presidency, prime ministership and parliamentary seats among sects |
| United Arab Emirates, Qatar | Islam | Many foreign workers, including many Hindus and Christians |
| Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia | Islam (almost everyone) | |

## Sub-Saharan Africa

Today about 30% of the world's Christians live in this region.

| Country | Largest religions | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nigeria | Islam and Christianity roughly half and half | Islam in the north, Christianity in the south |
| Ethiopia | Christianity (the Ethiopian Orthodox Church and others, about two-thirds) | Islam about one-third. A Christian country since the 4th century |
| Kenya | Christianity (about 86%) | Islam about 11% (the coast and northeast). 2019 census |
| Tanzania | Christianity and Islam | More Christians. Zanzibar is almost all Muslim |
| Malawi | Christianity (about 77%) | Islam about 14% (toward the south of the lake). 2018 census |
| Democratic Republic of the Congo | Christianity (over 90%) | Catholic and Protestant, the Kimbanguist Church |
| South Africa | Christianity (about 80%) | African-initiated independent churches (the Zion Christian Church and others) |
| Senegal, Mali, Somalia, Sudan | Islam | |
| Benin, Togo | Christianity, Islam, Vodun | Vodun (a traditional religion) is a national holiday (Benin, Chapter 11) |

- In Africa, many people follow Christianity or Islam while also keeping the old belief in honoring ancestral spirits ([Africa Textbook](https://yusuke1090.github.io/textbooks/?b=africa))

## Examples of countries divided by religion

| Country | How it is divided |
| --- | --- |
| Nigeria | Islam in the north, Christianity in the south. In elections, the president and vice-president are often paired from different religions |
| Lebanon | Recognizes 18 sects and divides political offices among them |
| India | Hindu majority, about 200 million Muslims. Some regions have mostly Sikhs (Punjab) or Christians (the northeast, such as Nagaland, and Kerala) |
| Malaysia | Ethnicity and religion overlap (Malays are Muslim; ethnic Chinese Buddhist, Taoist or Christian; ethnic Indians Hindu) |
| Bosnia and Herzegovina | Bosniaks are Muslim, Serbs Orthodox, Croats Catholic |
| Northern Ireland (UK) | Catholics and Protestants clashed, and the conflict continued until the 1998 peace agreement |
