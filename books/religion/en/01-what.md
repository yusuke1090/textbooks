# 1. What is religion?

## What is religion?

A religion is a system in which people believe in something beyond human power (gods, buddhas, spirits, the laws of the universe and so on), decide how to live, pray and hold ceremonies based on that belief, and connect with others who share the same faith. Giving answers to "what happens when we die?", "what do we live for?" and "what is right conduct?" is also a major role of religion.

Many religions have parts like the following.

| Part | Contents | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What is believed in | Gods, buddhas, ancestors, spirits | The one God of Judaism, Christianity and Islam; the gods of Hinduism; the buddhas of Buddhism |
| Teachings and scriptures | Collections of the words of God or the teachings of a founder | The Bible, the Qur'an (Koran), the Vedas, sutras |
| Prayer and ritual | Prayers and festivals in set forms | Worship services, Mass, shrine and temple visits, Obon |
| Rules | Rules on food, clothing and behavior | Not eating pork, not eating meat, fasting |
| Community and places | The people who gather, and buildings | Churches, mosques, synagogues, temples, shrines |
| Life milestones | Birth, coming of age, marriage, death | Baptism, coming-of-age rites, weddings, funerals |

## Ways to classify religions

### By the number of gods

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monotheism | There is only one God | Judaism, Christianity, Islam, Sikhism |
| Polytheism | There are many gods | Hinduism, Shinto, the religions of ancient Greece and Rome |
| Not centered on gods | Values teachings, enlightenment and how people live more than gods | Early Buddhism, Jainism, Confucianism |

- Some religions do not fit neatly. Hinduism has many gods, but many people also think "every god is a manifestation of one great being"

### By region of origin

| Group | Where it began | Religions | What they share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Abrahamic religions | The Middle East (today's Israel and Palestine, the Arabian Peninsula) | Judaism → Christianity → Islam | Believe in the same one God, and regard Abraham as the ancestor of their faith. Believe God conveyed his word to people (revelation), and value scripture |
| Indian religions | India | Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism | The ideas of rebirth (samsara) and that actions determine later results (karma) |
| East Asian religions | China, Japan | Confucianism, Taoism, Shinto, Chinese and Japanese Buddhism | Honoring ancestors. People often follow several teachings together |
| Ethnic religions, local faiths | All over the world | African traditional religions, Indigenous faiths and others | Honoring nature and ancestral spirits |

### Whether there is a founder

- **Founded religions**: the founder is clearly known. Christianity (Jesus), Islam (Muhammad), Buddhism (the Buddha), Sikhism (Nanak) and others. They spread easily beyond one people and are also called "world religions"
- **Ethnic religions**: no founder; they formed over a long time within a people's way of life. Judaism, Hinduism, Shinto and others

## The number of believers in the world

These are 2020 figures from a study published in 2025 by the Pew Research Center in the United States.

| Religion | Number | Share of world population | Change since 2010 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Christianity | About 2.3 billion | 28.8% | Numbers grew, but the share fell |
| Islam | About 2.0 billion | 25.6% | Growing fastest |
| No religion (unaffiliated) | About 1.9 billion | 24.2% | The share rose a little |
| Hinduism | About 1.2 billion | 14.9% | Share unchanged |
| Buddhism | About 320 million | 4.1% | The only religion whose numbers fell |
| Judaism | About 15 million | 0.2% | Grew a little |
| Others (Sikhism, Taoism, Jainism, folk religions and so on) | — | 2.2% | Share unchanged |

![The largest religion and denomination in each region. Reds are Christianity, green Islam, yellow Hinduism, purple Buddhism, gray no religion](images/world-religions-map.jpg)

- Three out of four people in the world say they believe in some religion
- 80% of the world's people live in a country where their own religion is the majority
- By 2020, the region with the most Christians had changed from Europe to **sub-Saharan Africa**
- Muslims have a high proportion of children (one in three is under 15), so their numbers are expected to keep growing

## More people have "no religion"

- People with no religion are numerous in China (about 1.3 billion, 90% of the population), Japan, the United States and Europe. China alone accounts for about two-thirds of the world's unaffiliated
- In Europe and North America, more people born into Christian families answer "no religion" as adults
- In 2020, the unaffiliated passed half the population in the Netherlands, Uruguay and New Zealand
- However, people who answer "no religion" often visit graves, join festivals, carry amulets and pray to ancestors. Japan and China are typical examples (Chapters 9 and 10)

## How to read this book

- Chapter 2 looks at which religions are largest in each country
- Chapters 3–11 summarize each religion: "overview", "origins and history", "what is believed", "rules and events" and "today"
- Chapter 12 covers the relationship of religion with politics and conflict, and what to be careful of at work and when traveling
- Religious figures vary greatly with how they are counted (what people say about themselves, or national or religious registrations). The figures in this book are rough guides. Sources are collected in Appendix B
- Religions are described in the form "believers think this way". This is not a book to decide which religion is right
