# 2. Writing

## The Writing test

| | Task 1 | Task 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What you write | A description of a graph, table, diagram, map or process | An essay giving your opinion on a social topic |
| Length | **150+ words** | **250+ words** |
| Suggested time | 20 minutes | 40 minutes |
| Weight | 1 | **2** (twice Task 1) |

- You write both in 60 minutes. Task 2 is worth more, so many people **write Task 2 first**. Either order is fine
- Writing too few words lowers your Task Achievement / Task Response score. Writing far more does not raise it (aim for about 170–190 words in Task 1 and 260–300 in Task 2)
- Do not use bullet points; write everything in **sentences and paragraphs**. You do not need a title
- On the computer test, the word count is shown on screen. On paper, practise until you know how many words you write per line, so you can count quickly

## The four marking criteria

| Criterion | What it checks | Band 7 looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Task Achievement** (Task 1) / **Task Response** (Task 2) | Whether you answer the question | Task 1: a **clear overview** of the main trends or differences. Task 2: answers **all parts** of the question, with a **clear position throughout** |
| **Coherence and Cohesion** | Organisation and linking | Ideas **progress logically**, and each paragraph has a central idea. Linking words are mostly used well, though sometimes overused or underused |
| **Lexical Resource** | Vocabulary | Enough range, with **less common words** and collocations. Only occasional mistakes in word choice or spelling (chapter 1) |
| **Grammatical Range and Accuracy** | Range and accuracy of grammar | **A variety of complex structures**, with **frequent error-free sentences**. Good control of grammar and punctuation |

- The four criteria carry equal weight. One high score cannot make up for low ones
- Without an **overview** in Task 1, Task Achievement is usually capped at 5 or 6. You need it for Band 7

## Task 1: describing graphs and diagrams

### Types of task

- **Line graph**: change over time
- **Bar chart** and **table**: comparing items
- **Pie chart**: proportions
- **Process diagram**: the steps of how something is made or works (use the passive)
- **Map**: how a place changed from past to present

Whatever the type, write **only what the visual shows**. Do not guess reasons or give your opinion.

### Four paragraphs

| Paragraph | Content | Length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Introduction | **Paraphrase** the question: what the visual shows, in one sentence | 1 sentence |
| 2. Overview | About two of the **biggest trends or differences**. No detailed numbers | 2 sentences |
| 3. Body 1 | Features of the first group, compared with numbers | 3–4 sentences |
| 4. Body 2 | Features of the second group, compared with numbers | 3–4 sentences |

- Starting the overview with `Overall, …` makes it obvious to the examiner
- You do not need every number. Choose **what stands out**: the highest and lowest, big changes, and points where lines cross
- Watch your tenses. Use the **past tense** for past data, and `is expected to` / `is predicted to` for forecasts

### Sample task and model answer

![A line graph for Task 1 practice (made-up data)](images/task1-milk.svg)

> The line graph shows the average amount of milk consumed per person in three countries between 2000 and 2020. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant. Write at least 150 words.

Model answer (164 words):

> The line graph compares the average amount of milk drunk per person each year in three countries, A, B and C, between 2000 and 2020.
>
> Overall, consumption in Country A was by far the highest throughout the period, although it declined steadily. By contrast, Country C saw a dramatic rise, while the figure for Country B changed relatively little.
>
> In 2000, a person in Country A drank around 110 litres of milk a year, almost three times the figure for Country B (40 litres) and eleven times that for Country C (10 litres). Over the following two decades, consumption in Country A fell gradually, reaching 90 litres in 2020.
>
> Country C, on the other hand, experienced rapid growth. Its figure almost tripled to 28 litres by 2010 and continued to climb, overtaking Country B in 2015 and finishing at 40 litres. Meanwhile, consumption in Country B dipped slightly to 33 litres in 2015 before levelling off at around 34 litres in the final year.

What makes this answer good:

- The first paragraph paraphrases `shows the average amount of milk consumed` as `compares the average amount of milk drunk`
- The `Overall` paragraph sums up the main features: A is highest but falling, C rises sharply, B hardly changes
- It uses **comparison language**: `almost three times`, `eleven times`, `almost tripled`, `overtaking`
- It describes the size of changes precisely: `fell gradually`, `dipped slightly`, `levelling off`

## Task 2: writing your opinion

### Types of question

| Type | End of the question | What to write |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agree or disagree | To what extent do you agree or disagree? | Decide how far you agree and give two reasons |
| Two views | Discuss both views and give your own opinion. | Explain both views **and give your own opinion** |
| Advantages and disadvantages | Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages? | Compare them and say **which is greater** |
| Causes and solutions | What are the causes? What can be done? | The causes and the solutions |
| Two questions | (two direct questions) | Answer **both questions** |

- The most common mistake is **not answering every part of the question**. If you forget your opinion in "Discuss both views **and give your own opinion**", Task Response drops to 5 or 6
- Underline the key words in the question and check what you must answer before you start

### Using 40 minutes

1. **5 minutes: plan.** Decide your position and note the main idea and one example for each paragraph
2. **30 minutes: write.** Introduction → two body paragraphs → conclusion
3. **5 minutes: check.** Subject–verb agreement, articles (a / the), plural s, spelling

### Four paragraphs

| Paragraph | Content |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Introduction | **Paraphrase** the topic and state **your position** (thesis) clearly |
| 2. Body 1 | Topic sentence → explanation → **a specific example** |
| 3. Body 2 | Topic sentence → explanation → a specific example |
| 4. Conclusion | Restate your position in new words. Do not add new ideas |

- The first sentence of each paragraph (the **topic sentence**) says what the paragraph is about. Examiners follow your argument through these sentences
- Examples can come from your own country or job. If you use numbers, keep them general ("in many countries"); you do not need exact statistics
- **Do not change** your position from start to finish. Do not stop at "it depends"; say which side you lean towards

### Sample task and model answer

> Some people believe that robots and artificial intelligence will make farming jobs disappear. Others argue that technology will make farming more attractive to young people. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Model answer (279 words):

> Agriculture is changing rapidly as robots and artificial intelligence (AI) take over tasks that were once done by hand. While some people fear that this will eliminate farming jobs, others believe it will make the industry more appealing to younger generations. In my view, although automation will reduce the number of manual jobs, it is likely to create more skilled and attractive roles overall.
>
> On the one hand, it is undeniable that machines are replacing human labour on many farms. Milking robots, for instance, can milk a herd around the clock without a single worker present, and self-driving tractors can plough fields at night. As a result, farms need fewer people for repetitive physical work, and workers without technical skills may find it harder to secure employment in rural areas, where alternative jobs are often scarce.
>
> On the other hand, technology could solve one of farming's biggest problems: its unattractive image. Young people are often discouraged by long hours, hard physical labour and the lack of days off. If sensors monitor animals' health and drones spray crops, farmers can spend more time analysing data and less time on exhausting chores. This kind of work resembles a career in technology, which may persuade graduates to stay in or return to the countryside. Moreover, new roles such as maintaining robots and interpreting data require people with digital skills.
>
> In conclusion, while robots and AI will undoubtedly remove some traditional farming jobs, I believe their overall effect will be positive, because they make agriculture more efficient and more appealing to young people. Governments should therefore invest in training so that rural workers can benefit from these changes rather than be left behind.

What makes this answer good:

- The last sentence of the introduction (`In my view, …`) states a **clear position**, and the conclusion repeats it
- Body 1 explains the opposing view and Body 2 the view closer to the writer's: **both views** are covered
- **Specific examples** support the ideas (`Milking robots, for instance, …`)
- **A variety of complex sentences**: relative clauses (`where alternative jobs are often scarce`, `which may persuade …`), a conditional (`If sensors monitor …`) and `so that`
- **Less common words** and collocations from chapter 1: `eliminate`, `appealing`, `undeniable`, `secure employment`, `exhausting chores`

## Tips for Band 7

- **Mix in complex sentences**: relative clauses (which / who / where), conditionals (if / unless), the passive and clauses with `although`, once or twice per paragraph. Not every sentence needs to be long
- **Write more error-free sentences**: a correct sentence you are sure of scores better than a difficult one with mistakes
- **Avoid memorised phrases**: lines that fit any essay, such as `In this day and age` or `It is a well-known fact that`, are easy for examiners to spot and earn nothing
- **Do not repeat**: avoid copying the question's words or reusing the same word; use the paraphrases from chapter 1
- **Use written style**: `don't` → `do not`, `a lot of` → `a large number of`, `kids` → `children`, `get` → `obtain` / `become`

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correct | Point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The number of cows **have** increased. | The number of cows **has** increased. | The subject is `The number` (singular). `A number of cows have …` is plural |
| **Environment** is getting worse. | **The environment** is getting worse. | Use `the` for the natural environment |
| many **informations** | much **information** | Uncountable noun (chapter 1) |
| It is expensive, **however** it is useful. | It is expensive. **However,** it is useful. | Do not join two sentences with `however` (chapter 1) |
| **Although** it is cheap, **but** it is slow. | **Although** it is cheap, it is slow. | Do not use `although` and `but` together |
| people **who lives** in cities | people **who live** in cities | The verb after a relative pronoun agrees with the noun before it |
| In 2020, the price **increases** to … | In 2020, the price **increased** to … | Past data takes the past tense |

E: Task 1 needs at least 150. Task 2 counts twice as much as Task 1.

Q: In Task 1, what is the paragraph that sums up the biggest trends or differences called?
A: overview | an overview
E: Without an overview, Task Achievement is usually capped at 5 or 6. Start it with `Overall, …`.

Q: One of the four Writing criteria is "Grammatical Range and ___". What one word fills the gap?
A: Accuracy
E: The others are Task Achievement / Task Response, Coherence and Cohesion, and Lexical Resource.

Q: Which verb fills the gap: have or has? The number of cows ___ increased.
A: has
E: The subject is The number (singular), so has. With A number of cows (many cows), use have.

Q: In a "Discuss both views and give your own opinion" question, what must you write besides both views?
A: your own opinion | your opinion | opinion | my opinion
E: Forgetting it means not answering every part of the question, which lowers Task Response.
