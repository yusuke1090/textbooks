# 5. Listening

## The Listening test

- About **30 minutes**, **4 parts**, **40 questions**. You hear the recording **once only**
- On the paper test you write answers on the question paper as you listen, then get **10 minutes** at the end to transfer them to the answer sheet. On the computer test you get only **2 minutes** at the end to check
- Before each part there is time to read the questions (and short pauses between question sets within a part)
- **Spelling mistakes make an answer wrong.** British and American spellings are both accepted
- Band 7 needs **about 30 correct answers out of 40** (it varies a little between tests)
- Speakers have a range of accents: British, Australian, New Zealand, American, Canadian and others

| Part | Speakers | Context | Typical questions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Part 1** | Two people talking | Everyday life (bookings, applications, enquiries) | Form completion (names, dates, numbers, addresses) |
| **Part 2** | One person | Everyday life (facilities, tours, events) | Multiple choice, maps and plans |
| **Part 3** | Two to four people | Education and research (students, or a student and tutor) | Multiple choice, matching |
| **Part 4** | One person | A university lecture | Note completion (no break in the middle) |

- The parts get harder as you go. Band 7 depends on scoring reliably in Parts 1 and 2, and on how far you can push Parts 3 and 4

## Question types

| Type | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| **Form / note / table / flow-chart completion** | Fill gaps in a form, notes, table or flow chart |
| **Sentence / summary completion** | Fill gaps in sentences or a summary |
| **Multiple choice** | Choose one answer (or two or more) |
| **Matching** | Match people or things with descriptions |
| **Plan / map / diagram labelling** | Label places on a map or diagram |
| **Short-answer questions** | Answer briefly |

- Gap fills have **word limits** such as `NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER`. Going over the limit makes the answer wrong
- You may write numbers as digits (`15` rather than `fifteen`). If the symbol or unit (`£`, `%`) is already printed on the question paper, you do not need to write it
- Questions follow **the order of the recording** almost exactly

## Preparing before you listen (prediction)

Use the reading time to:

1. **Underline keywords**: names, dates, places and other words that tell you where you are
2. **Predict the kind of word**: a number, a name, a noun or an adjective? Singular or plural?
   - `The tour costs £ ____ per person.` → a number (a price)
   - `Visitors should bring their own ____.` → a noun (something to carry)
3. **Expect paraphrases**: `cost` in the question may be `price`, `fee` or `charge` in the recording (chapter 1)
4. For multiple choice, look at **how the options differ** before you listen

## Distractors and signposting

### Corrections (distractors)

Speakers often **change what they first said**. Write the final answer.

- Five adults. **Oh, sorry**, my sister wants to come too, **so that's six**. → the answer is 6
- **I was going to** come on Friday, **but** … → not Friday
- `Actually`, `No, wait`, `Sorry, I mean` and `Let me check` signal that the answer is about to change

### Signposting words

In Parts 2 and 4, the speaker **signals a move to the next point**. Listen for these so you do not lose your place.

| Job | Words |
| --- | --- |
| Starting | First of all, … / To begin with, … |
| Moving on | Moving on to … / Next, … / Now let's turn to … |
| Example | For instance, … / Take … as an example. |
| Key point | The key point is … / What's important here is … |
| Ending | Finally, … / To sum up, … |

## Catching spellings and numbers

- **The alphabet**: practise pairs that are easy to confuse: `A / E / I`, `G / J`, `B / V`, `M / N`, `P / B`
- `double`: `double L` is `LL`, `double four` is `44`
- In phone numbers, `oh` means **0** (zero)
- **Dates**: `the thirteenth of March` / `March the thirteenth` → write `13 March` or `13th March`. Tell `thirteen` (13) from `thirty` (30) by the stress (thir**TEEN** / **THIR**ty)
- **Prices**: `eighteen pounds fifty` → `£18.50`
- **Postcodes**: British postcodes mix letters and numbers, such as `CB2 1TN`

```listen
# Numbers and spelling practice
The surname is Pemberton. That's P, E, M, B, E, R, T, O, N.
The postcode is C B two, one T N.
My reference number is four seven double nine two.
The meeting is on the thirtieth of November.
The ticket costs fifteen pounds fifty.
```

- Press ▶ and write what you hear, then check with "Show script". If it is too fast, press "Slower"

## Part 4 practice: completing lecture notes

```listen
# Lecture: cows and rest
Lecturer: Today I'd like to look at something that dairy farmers often underestimate, and that is rest. A cow typically spends between ten and fourteen hours a day lying down, and research suggests that cows which rest longer tend to produce more milk. Why should that be? The main reason is blood flow. When a cow lies down, more blood passes through the udder, and of course, milk is made from blood. Lying down also gives the hooves a chance to dry and recover, which reduces lameness. So, what does this mean in practice? First, stalls must be comfortable, with soft, dry bedding. Second, cows should not have to wait too long to be milked, because time spent standing in a queue is time not spent resting. Finally, overcrowding should be avoided, since cows lower in the social order will often give up their stalls to more dominant animals.
```

As you listen, try to fill each gap in the notes below with one word. Check your answers with "Show script".

- Cows lie down for 10–14 hours a day
- Lying down increases ______ flow to the udder
- Rest helps the ______ dry and recover → less lameness
- In practice: comfortable stalls with soft, dry ______; short waiting times for milking; avoid ______

## Exercises

Listen to the 🔊 audio below and answer Q1–5. You may listen twice here, but in the real test you hear it only once.

```listen
# Part 1 practice: booking a farm tour
Woman: Good morning, Greenhill Farm Tours. How can I help you?
Man: Hi, I'd like to book a tour of the dairy farm, please.
Woman: Certainly. Can I take your surname?
Man: Yes, it's Hartley. H, A, R, T, L, E, Y.
Woman: Thank you, Mr Hartley. And which day would you like to come?
Man: Is Friday the twelfth possible?
Woman: I'm afraid Friday is fully booked, but we still have places on Saturday the thirteenth.
Man: Saturday's fine.
Woman: And how many people will there be?
Man: Five adults. Oh, sorry, my sister wants to come too, so that's six.
Woman: Six adults, then. The tour costs eighteen pounds per person, and that includes a glass of fresh milk and a cheese tasting at the end.
Man: Great. Do we need to bring anything?
Woman: Just boots, please. The yard can get quite muddy.
```

```quiz
Q: What is the caller's surname? Spell it correctly.
A: Hartley
E: He spells it out: "H, A, R, T, L, E, Y". Spelling names is common in Part 1.

Q: On what date is the tour booked (the day number)?
A: 13 | 13th | thirteenth
E: Friday the 12th is full, so it is Saturday the 13th. The first number you hear is not always the answer.

Q: How many people are going (a number)?
A: 6 | six
E: He says "Five" and then corrects himself: "so that's six". The corrected number is the answer.

Q: How much does the tour cost per person, in pounds (a number)?
A: 18 | £18
E: "eighteen pounds per person". Tell eighteen (18) from eighty (80) by the stress (eigh**TEEN** / **EIGH**ty).

Q: What are visitors asked to bring? (ONE WORD ONLY)
A: boots
E: "Just boots, please." The yard can get muddy.
```
