# 4. Speaking

## The Speaking test

You talk one-to-one with an examiner for **11–14 minutes**. The conversation is recorded.

| Part | Time | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Part 1** | 4–5 minutes | Short questions about yourself (work, studies, hometown, hobbies and so on) |
| **Part 2** | 3–4 minutes | Prepare for **1 minute**, then talk alone for **1–2 minutes** on a topic card (cue card). One or two short questions follow |
| **Part 3** | 4–5 minutes | A discussion of social and abstract questions linked to the Part 2 topic |

- In Part 2 you get paper and a pencil for notes. The examiner stops you after 2 minutes
- The examiner can repeat a question but will not explain words in Part 1 (in Part 3 they may rephrase)
- The content of your answers (whether your opinion is right, or whether it is true) is not marked. **Only your English** is marked

## The four marking criteria

| Criterion | What it checks | Band 7 looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fluency and Coherence** | Smoothness and logical flow | You **speak at length** without noticeable effort. Sometimes you pause to find words or correct yourself. You use linking words with some flexibility |
| **Lexical Resource** | Vocabulary | You use vocabulary **flexibly** across topics, including less common and idiomatic words, and you can **paraphrase** |
| **Grammatical Range and Accuracy** | Range and accuracy of grammar | You use **complex structures** with some flexibility and produce **many error-free sentences**, though some mistakes remain |
| **Pronunciation** | Pronunciation | You are **easy to understand** throughout, using stress, intonation and chunking well. Your accent does not cause the listener difficulty |

- Having an accent is not penalised. What matters is **whether the listener understands you without effort**
- Very short answers give you no chance to show fluency, vocabulary or grammar

## Part 1: talking about yourself

- Answer each question in **two or three sentences**. "Answer → reason → example" naturally makes answers longer
- Do not stop at "Yes." or "I like it." But do not talk for a whole minute either; the examiner needs to move on
- Common topics: work or studies, hometown, home, hobbies, food, weather, smartphones, holidays, music, sport

```listen show
# Part 1 example
Examiner: Do you work or are you a student?
Candidate: I work. I'm an engineer at a small company that makes sensors for dairy farms, so I spend a lot of my time writing software and testing devices on real farms. It's quite demanding, but I really enjoy seeing my work being used.
Examiner: Do you like cooking?
Candidate: To be honest, I'm not much of a cook. On weekdays I usually make simple things like curry or stir-fried vegetables, because I don't have much time. But at weekends I sometimes try out a new recipe, mainly as a way to relax.
```

- **Natural spoken phrases** such as `To be honest`, `not much of a cook` and `mainly as a way to relax` make the answers sound fluent
- The first answer follows "answer (I work) → detail (what job) → feeling (demanding but enjoyable)"

## Part 2: talking alone for 1–2 minutes

### A sample cue card

> **Describe a piece of technology that you find useful.**
>
> You should say:
>
> • what it is
> • when you started using it
> • how you use it
>
> and explain why you find it useful.

### One minute to prepare

- Note **keywords only** for each of the four points (do not write sentences)
- The last point, `explain why …`, is the most important. Prepare two reasons
- Following the card's order is fine. Going past → present → feelings also lets you show a range of tenses

### Model answer

```listen show
# Part 2 example (about 1 minute 50 seconds)
I'd like to talk about a tiny computer board called the Raspberry Pi Pico. It's about the size of a stick of chewing gum, and it's surprisingly cheap.
I started using it about a year ago, when I was looking for a way to measure the temperature in a cowshed without buying an expensive commercial system. A colleague recommended it, so I ordered one online and taught myself how to program it.
As for how I use it, I connect small sensors to it, for example a sensor that measures temperature and humidity, and I write programs in C++ that read the data every few seconds and send it to my computer. At first nothing worked, and I spent hours checking the wiring, but gradually I got the hang of it.
The main reason I find it so useful is that it lets me turn an idea into a working prototype very quickly. If I want to test whether a sensor can detect when a cow is lying down, I can build something in an afternoon. It has also taught me a lot about electronics, which has made me much more confident at work.
So, although it's just a small, inexpensive board, it has had a big impact on the way I work.
```

- `As for how I use it`, `The main reason … is that …` and `So, although …` **clearly signal** each move to a new point on the card
- `got the hang of it` (learned how to do it) is an example of idiomatic language
- If you run out of ideas, add **examples or feelings** (When I first …, I felt …). Paraphrasing and carrying on scores better than falling silent

## Part 3: discussion

- The questions widen the Part 2 topic to **society as a whole** (for example, has technology made people happier, and how will it change)
- Answering "opinion → reason → example → the other side" shows both depth and complex sentences
- Talk **in general terms** (many people, in my country, in the future), not only about your own experience
- Phrases for opinions and predictions: `I'd say …`, `On balance, I think …`, `I suspect …`, `It's likely that …`, `That said, …`, `It depends on …`

```listen show
# Part 3 example
Examiner: Do you think technology has made people's lives easier or more stressful?
Candidate: I'd say it's a bit of both, but on balance I think it has made life easier. For example, online banking means we no longer have to queue at a bank. On the other hand, because we're always connected, it's harder to switch off from work, and I think that's a major source of stress for many people.
Examiner: How might technology change farming in the future?
Candidate: I suspect we'll see far more automation. Robots are already milking cows on many farms, and I imagine drones and sensors will become standard equipment, even on small farms. That said, I don't think farmers will disappear. Their role will probably shift from physical work to managing data and machines.
```

## Buying time and asking for repetition

| Situation | What to say |
| --- | --- |
| You need a moment | That's an interesting question. / Let me think about that for a moment. |
| A topic you have never considered | I've never really thought about that, but I suppose … |
| You cannot find a word | What's the word … / I mean … |
| You did not hear the question | Sorry, could you repeat the question, please? |
| You did not understand it (Part 3) | Do you mean …? / Could you rephrase that, please? |

- Linking with phrases like these sounds more fluent than a long `Um … er …`
- Asking for repetition is not penalised. It is better than answering a different question

## Pronunciation points for speakers of Japanese

- **Do not add vowels**: say `desk`, not "desku", and `milk`, not "miruku"
- Distinguish **r and l**, **th** (θ in `think`, ð in `this`) and **v and b** (`very` / `berry`)
- **Word stress**: `deVELop`, `TECHnology` (not techNOlogy), `ecoNOMic`. Stressing the wrong syllable makes words hard to recognise
- **Sentence stress**: stress the important words (nouns, verbs, adjectives) and make `a`, `the` and `to` weak and short
- **Chunking**: pause between units of meaning. `The main reason I find it useful / is that it lets me / turn an idea into a prototype.`
- **Intonation**: do not keep it flat. Fall at the end of statements; rise for yes/no questions
- Play the 🔊 model answers on "Slower" and **copy them aloud** (shadowing) to build stress and chunking

## How to practise

- **Record** yourself answering questions and listen back. Note pauses, repeated words and grammar mistakes
- For Part 2, practise one card a day with a timer: 1 minute to prepare, 2 minutes to speak
- Put words from chapter 1 into your own Part 1 answers and say them aloud
