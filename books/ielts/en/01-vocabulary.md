# 1. Vocabulary

This book is for getting **Band 7** in IELTS (Academic). Chapter 1 builds your vocabulary, and chapters 2–5 teach how to tackle Writing, Reading, Speaking and Listening. Examples and model answers are in English. Switch to 日本語 to read the explanations in Japanese.

## IELTS and band scores

| Skill | Time | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Listening | About 30 minutes | 4 parts, 40 questions (chapter 5) |
| Reading | 60 minutes | 3 long texts, 40 questions (chapter 3) |
| Writing | 60 minutes | Task 1 (150+ words) and Task 2 (250+ words) (chapter 2) |
| Speaking | 11–14 minutes | One-to-one with an examiner, 3 parts (chapter 4) |

- Each skill gets a **band from 0 to 9** (in steps of 0.5). The **overall band** is the average of the four, rounded to the nearest half band. An average ending in .25 rounds up to .5, and one ending in .75 rounds up to the next whole band
  - Example: Listening 7.5, Reading 7.0, Writing 6.5, Speaking 7.0 → average 7.0 → overall **7.0**
  - Example: 7.0, 7.0, 6.5, 6.5 → average 6.75 → overall **7.0**
- Band 7 is a "**good user**": you handle complex language well and understand detailed reasoning, with occasional errors or awkward phrases. It is a common requirement for postgraduate study, universities abroad and work visas
- In Listening and Reading, **about 30 correct answers out of 40** gives Band 7 (it varies a little between tests)

## What Band 7 vocabulary means

In Writing and Speaking, vocabulary (**Lexical Resource**) is a quarter of the score. Band 7 means:

- You have enough range to say things **with some flexibility and precision**
- You use **less common words**, with some awareness of **collocation** (which words go together) and of style (written versus spoken)
- You make only occasional mistakes in word choice or spelling

You do not need lots of difficult words. What matters is using **ordinary words in the right combinations, without repeating yourself**.

## How to learn words

- **Learn collocations**: not just `problem`, but `tackle a problem` and `a growing problem` — learn the words that go with it
- **Keep three paraphrases**: repeating the same word does not raise your vocabulary score. For `important`, keep `crucial`, `vital` and `essential` ready (see "Paraphrase sets" below)
- **Learn word families**: `economy` (noun), `economic` (adjective), `economically` (adverb), `economise` (verb) — so you can build sentences in different ways
- **Make your own sentences**: one sentence about your own life (job, hometown, interests) with each new word makes it ready for Speaking
- **Learn the spelling**: in Listening and Reading, a misspelt answer is wrong. `environment`, `government`, `accommodation` and `necessary` come up often and are easy to misspell

These words describe changes in numbers for Writing Task 1 (chapter 2). Learn both the verb and the noun.

| Movement | Verbs | Nouns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| go up | rise, increase, grow, climb | a rise, an increase, growth |
| go up suddenly | soar, surge, rocket | a surge |
| go down | fall, decrease, decline, drop | a fall, a decrease, a decline, a drop |
| go down suddenly | plummet, plunge | a plunge |
| go up and down | fluctuate | a fluctuation |
| stay level | level off, stabilise, plateau | a plateau |
| reach the top | peak (at …) | a peak |
| reach the bottom | hit a low, bottom out | a low |

| Degree | Adjectives | Adverbs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a lot | significant, substantial, dramatic | significantly, substantially, dramatically |

| a little | slight, marginal | slightly, marginally |

- Verb sentence: Milk consumption **rose sharply** from 2010 to 2015.
- Noun sentence: There was **a sharp rise** in milk consumption from 2010 to 2015.
- Approximate numbers: **roughly / approximately** 40%, **just over / just under** half, **a quarter**, **two-thirds**, **the vast majority**

## Linking words

| Job | Words | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adding | moreover, furthermore, in addition | **Moreover**, robots can work at night. |
| Contrasting | however, nevertheless, on the other hand | **However**, the cost remains high. |
| Comparing | whereas, while | Sales rose in Asia, **whereas** they fell in Europe. |
| Result | therefore, consequently, as a result | **Consequently**, many farmers left the industry. |
| Reason | due to, owing to (+ noun) | **Due to** the drought, yields fell. |
| Example | for instance, for example, such as | **For instance**, Denmark taxes sugary drinks. |
| Concession | although, even though, despite (+ noun) | **Despite** the cost, the system is popular. |
| Summing up | in conclusion, overall | **Overall**, the benefits outweigh the drawbacks. |

- Put `however` at the start or in the middle of a sentence, separated by **commas**. Joining two sentences with just `however` is wrong (× It is cheap, however it is slow.)
- `despite` and `due to` are followed by a **noun**; `although` and `because` are followed by a **clause** (despite **the rain** / although **it rained**)
- Putting a linking word in every sentence lowers your score (overuse). Use them at the start of paragraphs and where the argument changes direction

## Easily confused words

| Pair | How to use them |
| --- | --- |
| affect / effect | affect is a verb; effect is usually a noun. The drought **affected** yields. / It **had an effect on** yields. |
| economic / economical | economic = about the economy; economical = saving money. **economic** growth / an **economical** car |
| rise / raise | rise has no object; raise needs one. Prices **rose**. / They **raised** prices. |
| fewer / less | fewer for countable nouns, less for uncountable. **fewer** farms / **less** milk |
| advice / advise | advice is an uncountable noun; advise is a verb. some **advice** / I **advise** you to … |
| lose / loose | lose = stop having; loose = not tight. |
| percent / percentage | after a number use percent (20 **percent**); without a number use percentage (a large **percentage**) |
| information / research / equipment | uncountable nouns. × informations, × researches, × equipments |

Q: Listening 7.5, Reading 7.0, Writing 6.0, Speaking 6.5. What is the overall band?
A: 7 | 7.0
E: The average is 6.75, and .75 rounds up to the next whole band: 7.0.

Q: Which is the **verb** meaning "to influence": affect or effect?
A: affect
E: effect is usually a noun. The drought affected yields.

Q: Fill the gap with one word: There were ___ farms in the region than ten years ago. (fewer or less)
A: fewer
E: farms is countable, so fewer. Use less with uncountable nouns such as milk.

Q: "Deal with an issue" = ___ an issue. Which verb starting with a fills the gap?
A: address | addresses | addressing
E: address an issue / tackle a problem are useful collocations for Writing.

Q: Which verb starting with p means "fall suddenly" when describing numbers?
A: plummet | plunge | plummeted | plunged
E: plummet / plunge. For a sudden rise, use soar / surge.
