# 3. From seed rice to transplanting

## What you will learn

- Preparing the seed (salt-water selection, disinfection, soaking, sprouting)
- How to raise seedlings (seedling trays, young and middle-aged seedlings, dense seedlings)
- Plowing and puddling
- How transplanting is done
- Direct seeding

There is a Japanese saying, ***nae-hansaku*** (苗半作): "the seedling is half the crop". If you raise good seedlings, you are already halfway to success.

## Preparing the seed

| Task | How | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Salt-water selection** | Put the seed in salt water (specific gravity 1.13; for non-glutinous rice, about 2 kg of salt in 10 L of water) and throw away what floats | Choose only heavy seeds that are well filled |
| **Seed disinfection** | Soak in 60 °C water for 10 minutes (**hot-water disinfection**) or in a chemical (fungicide) | Prevent seed-borne diseases (bakanae disease, rice blast and others) |
| **Soaking** | Soak in water (10–15 °C) for 7–10 days. Water temperature × days = about 100 °C is the guide (for example, about 8 days at 12 °C) | Let the seed absorb water and get ready to sprout |
| **Sprouting** | Soak in 30–32 °C water for about a day | Make the sprouts come out evenly, just a little (**"pigeon breast"**: a white sprout about 1 mm long) |

- Farmers usually buy seed each year that was grown in disease-free **seed production fields** (paddies used only for producing seed). If you use rice from your own field as seed, diseases and mixing with other varieties increase
- Hot-water disinfection uses no pesticides, so it is often used for organic rice and reduced-pesticide rice. If the temperature or time is a little off, the seeds may not sprout, or may not be disinfected

## Raising seedlings

![Seedling trays lined up. One tray is 60 cm long and 30 cm wide](images/seedling-trays.jpg)

- Almost all transplanting today uses **tray-raised seedlings**. Put soil in plastic **seedling trays** (about 60 × 30 cm), sow the seed, and raise them in a greenhouse or field
- Steps
  1. Put bed soil (soil with fertilizer) in the tray and water it
  2. Sow the sprouted seed and cover it with soil
  3. Let them sprout at 30–32 °C for 2–3 days (**emergence**)
  4. Turn them green in weak light for 1–2 days (**greening**)
  5. Get them used to outdoor temperatures little by little to make them sturdy (**hardening**). Ventilate the greenhouse so that it does not go above 25 °C in the daytime

| Seedling type | Seed per tray (dry weight) | Days to raise | Leaf age at planting | Trays per 10 a |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Young seedlings (*chibyō*) | 150–180 g | 20–25 days | 2.0–2.5 leaves | 15–20 trays |
| Middle-aged seedlings (*chūbyō*) | 80–100 g | 30–35 days | 3.0–4.0 leaves | 20–25 trays |
| **Dense seedlings** (*mitsubyō*) | 250–300 g | 15–20 days | About 2.0 leaves | 6–8 trays |

- Young seedlings are the most common in Japan. Cold regions (such as Hokkaido) also use middle-aged seedlings, which tolerate cold, and **pot seedlings** raised one by one in small pots
- **Dense seedlings** are a method of sowing more seed and cutting the number of trays to about a third. It greatly reduces the work of carrying seedlings and the cost of greenhouses and materials, so it is spreading among large farms (a dedicated transplanter is needed)
- Seedling diseases: damping-off (a fungus), bakanae disease (seedlings grow tall and spindly), and seedlings wilting after heat followed by a sudden chill

## Plowing and puddling

![Puddling (rice terraces). A tractor stirs the soil of a flooded paddy and levels it](images/puddling.jpg)

- **Plowing** (*ta-okoshi*): in spring, the dry paddy is plowed with a tractor, mixing last year's straw and stubble into the soil. About 15 cm deep
- **Puddling** (*shirokaki*): a few days before transplanting, water is let in and the soil is stirred finely to make it muddy and level
  - Makes the paddy hold water better (fills the gaps)
  - Levels the surface so the water depth is even (if there are deep and shallow places, seedling growth and weeds become uneven)
  - Buries weeds in the soil and makes the soil soft and easy to plant in
- The guide is to keep differences in the field's height within ±2–3 cm. Recently there are also machines (levelers) that measure height with lasers or GNSS to level the field
- Care is needed not to let the muddy puddling water flow into rivers: do not let in too much water, and do not drain it right away

## Transplanting

![A riding rice transplanter. It carries seedling trays and plants many rows at once](images/transplanter.jpg)

- The usual way is to plant 6–8 rows at once with a riding **rice transplanter**. One machine can plant several hectares a day
- Planting guidelines

| Item | Guide |
| --- | --- |
| Row spacing | 30 cm |
| Hill spacing (between hills in the same row) | 15–20 cm (16–22 hills per m²) |
| Seedlings per hill | 3–5 |
| Planting depth | 2–3 cm |
| Water depth | 1–3 cm (shallower is easier to plant) |

- Too shallow and the seedlings float; too deep and tillering is delayed
- **Sparse planting** (*soshoku*), with wider hill spacing and fewer hills, means fewer seedling trays and easier work. But be careful that you do not end up with too few ears
- Transplanting is mostly in mid-to-late May in Hokkaido and Tohoku, late April to May in Kanto and Hokuriku, and June in western Japan. To avoid heat damage, some regions have recently shifted planting a little later

## Direct seeding

![Flooded direct seeding. Seed is sown straight into the paddy while puddling](images/direct-seeding.jpg)

- A method that sows seed directly into the paddy without raising seedlings. It removes the work of raising seedlings and transplanting, greatly reducing spring work
- There are two ways

| Way | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Flooded direct seeding** (*tansui*) | Sow into a puddled paddy with water in it. Seed coated with iron powder (iron coating) is less likely to be eaten by birds or to float |
| **Dry direct seeding** (*kanden*) | Sow into a dry paddy before flooding, with a seed drill like wheat or soybeans. Water is let in after the seedlings come up. Field machinery can be used, so it suits large farms |

- Difficulties: uneven emergence, many weeds, plants fall over easily, damage from birds and apple snails. Yields are often a little lower than with transplanting
- It is only a few percent of Japan's paddies, but it is slowly increasing as the number of workers falls. There is also a method of sowing seed by drone (Chapter 12)
