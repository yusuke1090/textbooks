# 4. Water and fertilizer in the paddy

In rice growing, people say "**water is fertilizer**". Walking around the paddies every morning and changing the water depth is an important daily job for rice farmers.

![Lush green paddies before the rainy season (Niigata)](images/green-paddy.jpg)

## Water depth by stage

| Stage | Water | Aim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| After transplanting until rooting (about a week) | **Deep water** (4–6 cm) | Protect the seedlings from cold and wind, and let them take root (rooting) |
| Tillering stage | **Shallow water** (2–3 cm) | Raise the water and soil temperature to increase tillering |
| Once enough productive tillers are secured (30–40 days after transplanting) | **Midseason drainage** (drain for 7–10 days) | Stop tillering and strengthen the roots |
| After midseason drainage to panicle initiation | **Intermittent irrigation** (alternate letting water in and letting it drain naturally) | Send oxygen to the roots |
| Meiosis stage (10–12 days before heading) | **Deep water** (15–20 cm) when it is cold | Protect the young ear from cold air (against cold damage) |
| Heading and flowering | **Flooded** (never let it dry out) | The time when rice needs water most |
| Ripening | Intermittent irrigation | Keep the roots and leaves healthy for longer |
| 25–30 days after heading | **Final drainage** (drain the water) | Dry the paddy so the combine can get in at harvest |

- If the final drainage is too early, ripening suffers and chalky or cracked grains increase. If it is too late, the paddy is muddy and the combine cannot get in
- In hot years, water temperature is lowered by keeping cold water flowing (**flow-through irrigation**) or changing the water at night, to reduce heat damage
- Water in and out is adjusted with boards and plugs at the paddy's inlet (*minakuchi*) and outlet (*minajiri*). Automating this is what a **water management system** does (Chapter 12)

## Midseason drainage

- Once the productive tillers are in place, the water is drained once and the paddy is dried until small cracks appear on the surface
- Aims
  - Stop further (unproductive) tillering
  - Let oxygen into the soil to make the roots deep and strong
  - Release gases harmful to roots (such as hydrogen sulfide) that have built up in the soil
  - Firm up the paddy so machines can get in easily at harvest
  - Make the rice less likely to fall over

### Extending midseason drainage and methane

- The soil of a flooded paddy has no oxygen, and microbes living there produce **methane** (a greenhouse effect about 28 times that of carbon dioxide). Of the greenhouse gases from Japanese agriculture, methane from paddies is the largest share
- It is known that making midseason drainage **7 or more days longer** than usual cuts methane by about 30%
- This reduction can be sold as "**J-Credits**" (a system in which the government certifies how much greenhouse gas was cut, so it can be sold to companies), giving farmers a new income. There are also services where you record it with a smartphone app and they apply for many farms together (Chapter 12)
- But drying too long can reduce yields from lack of water, so do it while watching the condition of the paddy

## Fertilizer

### Nitrogen matters most

- What affects rice growth and yield most is **nitrogen** (N). Phosphate (P), potassium (K) and silica (Si, which makes rice sturdy) are also needed (Chapter 11)
- With too much nitrogen
  - The leaves are dark green, there are many stems and the plants are tall, so they **fall over easily** (lodging)
  - Diseases such as **rice blast** appear more easily
  - The rice's **protein** increases and its eating quality (taste) goes down
- With too little, there are fewer ears and grains, and yield falls. "Just right" is what matters

### When, and how much

| Fertilizer | Timing | Nitrogen (per 10 a, Koshihikari example) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Basal fertilizer** (*motogoe*) | Before transplanting (at plowing or puddling) | 2–4 kg |
| **Panicle fertilizer** (*hogoe*) | About 25 days before heading (young ear 1–2 mm) to 18 days before | 1–2 kg, once or twice |

- Koshihikari falls over easily, so it gets less nitrogen. Other varieties get about 6–10 kg in total
- Before applying panicle fertilizer, measure leaf color (with a color scale or chlorophyll meter), plant height and the number of stems to decide the amount. If the color is too dark, reduce the panicle fertilizer
- **Single-application fertilizer** (controlled-release fertilizer): fertilizer whose release speed is adjusted, so applying it once as basal fertilizer also covers the panicle fertilizer. It removes the work of top-dressing, so many farmers use it. However, the resin shells that coat the fertilizer (microplastics) flowing out into rivers and the sea have become a problem, and farmers are switching to fertilizers with shells that break down

## Rice straw and building up the soil

![Rice terraces in September (Niigata). They have turned golden, and harvest is near](images/terraces-sept.jpg)

- After harvest, plowing **rice straw** into the paddy increases the soil's organic matter (humus) and returns potassium and silica
- But if it is not plowed in during autumn and is left until spring, more methane and gases harmful to roots are produced as the straw rots in the paddy. **Plowing it in during autumn** is recommended
- Adding compost (cow manure and the like) to keep the soil fertile is also important. **Crop–livestock cooperation** (*kōchiku renkei*), in which dairy farmers and rice farmers exchange straw (cattle feed and bedding) and compost (fertilizer for paddies), is a good, long-standing system (see the Dairy Cow Textbook)
- Fertilizers rich in silica (calcium silicate and others) make rice leaves harder and the plants more resistant to disease and lodging
