# 10. Potatoes, sugar beet and corn

## What you will learn

- How potatoes are grown, and varieties for each use
- Sugar beet, and how sugar is made
- Corn (for feed, sweet corn and grain corn)

## Potatoes

![A potato field (Hokkaido). White and purple flowers bloom in July](images/potato-field.jpg)

### Uses and varieties

- Japan grows about 2.2 million tonnes of potatoes a year, about 80% of them in Hokkaido. In agricultural statistics they are called ***bareisho*** (馬鈴薯)
- The varieties are completely different depending on the use

| Use | Share (approx.) | Main varieties |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sold fresh (cooked at home) | About 30% | Danshaku (Irish Cobbler), May Queen, Kitaakari, Touya |
| Potato chips, French fries (processing) | About 30% | Toyoshiro, Snowden, Kitahime |
| Starch (*katakuriko* and others) | About 20% | Konafubuki (high in starch) |
| Seed potatoes | About 10% | ― |

### Seed potatoes

- Potatoes are not grown from seeds but by planting **potatoes** (seed potatoes). They produce potatoes with exactly the same traits as the parent
- Potatoes easily pick up viruses and diseases, which pass on to the next year. So the rule is that only **seed potatoes that have passed government inspection** may be planted (the Plant Protection Act). Seed potato fields are strictly managed for aphids, which carry viruses, and for disease
- To prevent the spread of cyst nematodes, seed potatoes are also inspected when they are moved from Hokkaido to Honshu

### The year (Hokkaido example)

| Timing | Work and growth |
| --- | --- |
| April | Expose seed potatoes to light to make them sprout (**light sprouting**). Cut large ones into pieces |
| Late April–May | **Planting** (potato planter). About 30 cm between plants, ridges about 75 cm apart |
| May–June | Shoots come up. **Hilling** (pile soil around the plants to make high ridges) |
| July | Around flowering, the potatoes start to swell underground |
| August | Stems and leaves start to turn yellow and die. Sometimes the tops are killed off before harvest |
| August–October | **Harvest** (potato harvester). Dig after the skins have hardened |

- Why **hilling** matters: if potatoes come out of the soil and are exposed to sunlight, they turn green and **solanine** (bitter and harmful to eat) increases
- Main diseases: **late blight** (long summer rains make leaves and stems die all at once; the cause of the Great Famine in 19th-century Ireland), common scab (scab-like marks on the surface; it appears more in alkaline soil, so keep the pH lowish), and virus diseases

## Sugar beet

![A sugar beet root. The big white root contains 16–17% sugar](images/sugar-beet-root.jpg)

- **Sugar beet** (*tensai*, beet) is a plant of the amaranth family that stores sugar in its root. In Japan it is grown **only in Hokkaido**
- Japan is about 35% self-sufficient in sugar. About 80% of domestic sugar comes from sugar beet, and the rest from **sugarcane** in Okinawa and Kagoshima

### How it is grown

- Hokkaido's summer is short, so most is grown by **paper pot transplanting**
  1. In March, sow seed in a greenhouse in seedling containers made of paper tubes joined like a honeycomb (**paper pots**)
  2. In late April–early May, plant the seedlings in the field with a transplanter (planted tube and all; the paper breaks down in the soil)
- To save work, **direct seeding** into the field is also increasing (about 30%). On the other hand, yields are a little lower, and the plants are vulnerable to wind and frost until they come up
- Harvest is in October–November. The roots are dug with a beet harvester and piled at the edge of the field, waiting their turn to be accepted by the factory

![A pile of harvested sugar beet. Left at the edge of the field until it is taken to the sugar factory](images/beet-pile.jpg)

### Until it becomes sugar

![Harvesting sugar beet in front of a sugar factory (Shibetsu)](images/beet-factory.jpg)

- Hokkaido has sugar factories run by three companies (Nippon Beet Sugar, Hokuren and Hokkaido Sugar)
- At the factory, the roots are sliced finely, the sugar is extracted with hot water, impurities are removed with lime, and the liquid is boiled down and crystallized. This is **beet sugar** (granulated sugar and others)
- The pulp left after extracting the sugar (**beet pulp**) becomes cattle feed
- The price of sugar beet is decided by weight and **sugar content**. Sugar content goes up with autumn weather that is sunny by day and cool at night
- Diseases: **Cercospora leaf spot** (spots on the leaves in hot, humid summers; sugar content drops sharply) and root rot. Cercospora leaf spot has been increasing with recent hot summers
- Because people are eating less sugar, the government is moving to reduce sugar beet planting and is gradually lowering the amount eligible for grants

## Corn

![A corn field (Hokkaido). Dent corn for feed grows taller than a person](images/corn-field.jpg)

| Type | Use | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dent corn** (for feed) | Silage for cattle (stems, leaves and ears chopped whole and fermented) | Hokkaido and dairy regions. See the feed chapters of the Dairy Cow Textbook |
| **Sweet corn** | Eaten by people (fresh, canned, frozen) | Mostly Hokkaido. Harvested in summer; sweetest when eaten right away |
| **Grain corn** | Only the kernels are dried and used as an ingredient in compound feed for livestock | A new conversion crop. The aim is to replace part of the roughly 15 million tonnes imported with domestic corn |

- Japan imports about 15 million tonnes of corn every year (mostly from the United States and Brazil). About two-thirds of it is livestock feed
- **Grain corn**, like wheat and soybeans, can be grown in bulk with large machines (general-purpose combines) and takes little labor, so it has been increasing on large farms and converted paddies in Hokkaido and Tohoku
- Corn roots grow deep, so including it in a rotation improves soil drainage and the growth of the next crop
- Watch out for the pest **fall armyworm** (an insect from the Americas that arrived in Japan in 2019)
