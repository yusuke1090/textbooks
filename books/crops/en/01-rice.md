## The rice plant and rice

- In Japanese, ***ine*** (稲) is the name of the plant, and ***kome*** (米) is what is inside its seed. In English, both are "rice"
- The names, in order
  - ***Momi*** (籾, paddy or rough rice): the seed as harvested. It has a hard shell (the hull, or husk) on the outside
  - ***Genmai*** (玄米, brown rice): with the hull removed. The thin brown skin (bran) and the germ remain
  - ***Seimai*** (精米, white rice): with the bran and germ polished off. The rice you normally cook and eat
- Rice is usually traded by **the weight of brown rice**. Prices are talked about as "so many yen per 60 kg (one *hyō*, or bale) of brown rice"

## Japonica and indica

| Type | Grain shape | When cooked | Main places |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Japonica** | Short and round | Sticky | Japan, the Korean Peninsula, northern China, California |
| **Indica** | Long and slender | Fluffy and separate | Southeast Asia, South Asia, southern China. About 80% of the world's rice |

- Almost all rice grown in Japan is japonica **non-glutinous rice** (*uruchi-mai*, everyday rice). There is also **glutinous rice** (*mochi-gome*, for rice cakes and *okowa*) and **sake rice** (*sakamai*, for Japanese sake)
- What makes rice sticky is a low amount of the starch **amylose**. The less amylose, the stickier (glutinous rice has almost 0%)

## Japan's rice in numbers

| Item | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| Harvest of rice for eating as a staple (2025 crop) | About 7.47 million tonnes (brown rice, measured with a 1.70 mm sieve). The highest since 2018 |
| Amount eaten per year (demand for staple rice) | Around 7 million tonnes. Falling a little every year |
| Amount one person eats per year | About 50 kg. Less than half of the roughly 118 kg in 1962 |
| Yield per 10 a | About 540 kg of brown rice (about 9 bales) |
| Farm businesses growing rice | About 710,000 (2020). Roughly halved in 20 years. The average age of growers is about 70 |

- 10 a (ares) is 1,000 m², and 1 ha is 10,000 m² (10 a × 10). One paddy field is usually 10–30 a (1 ha or more where fields have been enlarged and reshaped)
- Japan is almost 100% self-sufficient in rice. But under an agreement (minimum access), it imports about 770,000 tonnes of foreign rice every year, which is used for processing and animal feed
- In 2024–2025, rice ran short and its price rose sharply (the **Reiwa rice crisis**, Chapter 6)

## Main varieties

![White Koshihikari rice. The most widely grown variety in Japan](images/koshihikari.jpg)

| Variety | Where it is grown | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Koshihikari** | Niigata, Ibaraki, Tochigi, Chiba and all over Japan | About a third of the planted area. Sticky and sweet. Tall and falls over easily |
| Hitomebore | Miyagi, Iwate and others | Resistant to cold damage |
| Hinohikari | Kyushu, Kinki, Chugoku | The main variety of western Japan |
| Akitakomachi | Akita and others | Not too sticky; light taste |
| Nanatsuboshi, Yumepirika | Hokkaido | Hokkaido rice. Tolerant of cold |
| Tsuyahime, Seiten no Hekireki, Shinnosuke and others | Yamagata, Aomori, Niigata and others | Brand rice developed by each prefecture |
| Niji no Kirameki, Kinumusume | Kanto, western Japan and others | Tolerant of heat. Increasing recently |

- Many varieties are children or grandchildren of Koshihikari. They taste good, but they also tend to inherit its weakness to disease and heat, so farmers are now switching to **heat-tolerant varieties** (Chapter 3)

## The year's work, step by step

This is a rough example for central Honshu (Koshihikari, planted in early May). In Hokkaido and Kyushu, the timing shifts by about a month either way.

| Month | Work | Details |
| --- | --- | --- |
| March–April | Plowing the paddy (*ta-okoshi*), spreading fertilizer. Preparing the seed (salt-water selection, disinfection, soaking, sprouting) | Chapter 3 |
| April | Raising seedlings (*ikubyō*) | Chapter 3 |
| Late April–May | Flooding and leveling the soil (puddling, *shirokaki*), transplanting | Chapter 3 |
| May–June | Deeper water for rooting, shallower water for tillering. Herbicides | Chapters 4, 5 |
| Late June–July | Draining and drying the field (midseason drainage, *nakaboshi*) | Chapter 4 |
| July | The beginnings of the ear form (panicle initiation). Panicle fertilizer (*hogoe*) | Chapters 2, 4 |

| September | Harvesting (combine harvester), drying, hulling | Chapter 6 |
| October–November | Shipping and inspection. Plowing straw and stubble into the paddy | Chapter 6 |

## How a paddy field works

- A paddy is a field surrounded by **levees** (*aze*) that is filled with water and holds it
- Water is brought from rivers or reservoirs through **irrigation canals**, and used water flows out into **drainage canals**. The local canals have long been managed together by the whole community (land improvement districts)
- Under the plowed soil there is a hard layer (the **plow pan**, *sukidoko*) that holds the water. It helps the field keep water
- Flooding the field has many benefits
  - Weeds grow less (many weeds cannot sprout under water)
  - It protects the rice from winter cold and summer heat (water warms up and cools down slowly)
  - You can grow rice in the same place every year (there is almost no **continuous cropping injury**, because the water washes away diseases and harmful substances)
  - River water brings in nutrients
- On the other hand, the soil of flooded paddies gives off **methane** (a greenhouse gas) (Chapters 4 and 12)
