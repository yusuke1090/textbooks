# 8. Wheat and barley

![A wheat field in Furano (Hokkaido). In July it turns harvest gold](images/wheat-furano.jpg)

## Types of wheat and barley

| Crop | Main uses | Main growing areas |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Wheat** | Bread, udon, Chinese noodles, sweets, soy sauce | Hokkaido, Fukuoka, Saga, Gunma, Saitama |
| **Two-row barley** | Beer, shochu (malt) | Saga, Fukuoka, Tochigi |
| **Six-row barley** | Barley tea, pressed barley (rice cooked with barley) | Fukui, Toyama, Ishikawa, Ibaraki |
| **Naked barley** | Barley miso, pressed barley | Ehime, Kagawa |

- Wheat flour is divided by its amount of protein (the source of **gluten**) into bread flour (bread), all-purpose flour (udon) and cake flour (sweets)
- Japanese wheat used to be mainly all-purpose flour for udon, but varieties for bread and Chinese noodles are now increasing

## The year of autumn-sown wheat

Almost all Japanese wheat is **autumn-sown wheat**, sown in autumn and grown through the winter. It has the property that it cannot form ears without being exposed to winter cold (**vernalization**).

| Timing (Hokkaido) | Timing (Kanto, Kyushu) | Work and growth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mid-to-late September | November | **Sowing** (in rows with a seed drill; 5–10 kg per 10 a) |
| October–November | December–January | Sprouts, increases leaves and tillers, and enters winter |
| December–March (under snow) | January–February | **Overwintering**. In Kanto and elsewhere, **mugifumi** (treading on the wheat) presses down roots lifted by frost |
| April–May | February–March | Growth restarts as the snow melts (regrowth stage). Top-dressing |
| Early June | April | **Heading** and flowering |
| Late July | Late May–early June | **Harvest** (conventional combine). Drying and cleaning |

- Wheat in Honshu must be harvested before the rainy season. The early-summer season when the wheat turns gold is called ***bakushū*** ("the autumn of wheat")
- In Hokkaido there is also **spring-sown wheat** (such as "Haruyokoi" for bread), and "early-winter sowing", where seed is sown before the snow and overwinters under it
- After harvest, the wheat straw is rolled into bales and used for cattle bedding and feed

## Diseases and things to watch out for

### Fusarium head blight

![Wheat ears with Fusarium head blight. Part of the ear turns pink to reddish](images/fusarium.jpg)

- If rain continues around flowering, a fungus (*Fusarium*) gets into the ears, and the grains become whitish and shriveled
- The most dangerous part is the **mycotoxin** this fungus makes (deoxynivalenol, DON). It harms the health of people and livestock, and wheat above the government standard (1.0 mg per kg of wheat) cannot be used as food
- Countermeasures
  - Spray fungicide **at the start of flowering**, and again 7–10 days later (three times when it is rainy)
  - Do not harvest late. Dry quickly
  - After harvest, remove shriveled grains by specific gravity or color
- Others include **snow mold**, where leaves rot under the snow (Hokkaido), **powdery mildew**, which puts white powder on the leaves, and rusts

### Pre-harvest sprouting

- If ripe ears are rained on before harvest, the grains may sprout while still on the ear (**pre-harvest sprouting**)
- In sprouted wheat, the starch breaks down, and the quality of bread or noodles made from it drops sharply. It ends up selling at a low price (for feed)
- When rain continues at harvest time, farmers hurry to run their combines through the night during breaks in the weather

### Lodging and harvest

- With too much nitrogen, the ears get heavy and the plants fall over. Fallen wheat is hard to harvest, and sprouting and mold increase
- Harvest time is when the grain moisture has dropped to **25–30%** (in Hokkaido, when "the ear snaps by hand and a fingernail leaves a mark on the grain")
- After harvest, dry it right away at a drying facility to **12.5%** moisture or less (lower than rice)

## How domestic wheat is distributed

- Domestic wheat is sold by tender to flour milling companies through JA and others. Besides the price, the government's ***geta*** grant (Chapter 7) is paid to farmers according to the amount and quality (grade, protein and so on). The grant is often larger than what the farmer gets from the sale
- Foreign wheat is imported in bulk by the government and sold to flour millers (the government resale price). When international prices rose after the 2022 invasion of Ukraine, interest in domestic wheat grew
- Products that feature domestic wheat, such as "bread made with 100% Hokkaido wheat" and "udon made with local wheat", are also increasing
