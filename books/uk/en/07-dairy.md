## In numbers

| Item | UK (GB) | Japan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Number of dairy farms | 6,850 (April 2026) | About 12,000 (2024) |
| Cows in milk | About 1.59 million (April 2026). The lowest ever | About 1.33 million in total (including calves and heifers) |
| Per farm | About 230 cows in milk alone | ― |
| Milk produced | About 12.9 billion L (the year to July 2026) | About 7.3 million tonnes (about 7.1 billion L) |
| Milk price (received by farmers) | About 37 pence per L (August 2026, about 77 yen) | Around 120 yen per kg |

- GB means England, Scotland and Wales. Dairy in Northern Ireland (about 3,000 farms) is counted separately
- The number of dairy farmers **fell by about 15% in five years**. Even so, milk output is rising (because milk per cow is increasing and herds are getting bigger). As in Japan, the trend is "many farms quit, and the ones that remain get bigger"
- Milk per cow is about 8,000 L a year, a little less than Japan (about 9,000 kg). Many farms graze their cows and put more weight on producing cheaply than on producing a lot

## Grazing and housing

A feature of British dairy is how wide the range of systems is.

| System | What it is | Milk per cow (per year) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Grazing-based** (spring block calving) | All the cows calve together in spring, and are milked heavily in the season when grass grows. The New Zealand model. Low feed and machinery costs | 5,000–6,000 L |
| **Grazing in summer, housed in winter** | The most common system. Grazing from spring to autumn, silage in the barn in winter (about November–March) | 7,000–9,000 L |
| **Housed all year** | Fed a total mixed ration (TMR), sometimes milked three times a day. Cows calve all year round to keep milk output steady | 10,000–12,000 L or more |

- The southwest (Devon, Cornwall, Somerset), Wales and Northern Ireland have lots of rain and good grass, so grazing is common
- Barns usually have **cubicle housing** (freestalls), with a separate bed for each cow
- The main feed is **grass silage**. Further south, maize silage is also used

![A grass silage clamp (bunker). Cut grass is piled up, packed down and covered with a sheet to ferment](images/silage-clamp.jpg)

## Cattle breeds

![Jersey cows (Isle of Wight). High in butterfat, small in body](images/jersey-cows.jpg)

![Ayrshire cattle. Born in Scotland, hardy and good at grazing](images/ayrshire.jpg)

| Breed | Origin | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Holstein-Friesian | Netherlands, northern Germany (improved in Britain) | Gives the most milk. Most of Britain's dairy cows |
| Jersey | Jersey (Channel Islands) | Small, with high butterfat and protein. Suited to cheese and cream |
| Guernsey | Guernsey (Channel Islands) | Golden-colored milk (rich in carotene) |
| Ayrshire | Scotland | Hardy and long-lived. Suited to grazing |
| Crossbreds | ― | Holsteins crossed with Montbéliarde, Norwegian Red, Jersey and others. Aimed at hardiness and better fertility |

- On grazing-based farms, small New Zealand Friesians and Jersey crosses (Kiwi-cross) are popular
- Male calves from dairy cows, and calves sired with beef semen, are raised as beef cattle. About half of Britain's beef comes from the dairy herd

## Milking

![A rotary parlour. Cows step onto a turning platform and are milked during one rotation. For large herds](images/rotary-parlour.jpg)

- The **herringbone parlour** (cows stand at an angle) is the most common. Large herds use a **rotary**
- **Milking robots**: cows enter the robot by themselves whenever they like, and the robot finds the teats and milks them. About 10% of British dairy farmers use them, and **about 35% of newly installed milking systems are robots**. The reasons are labor shortages and the high cost of labor
  - The main makers are Lely (Netherlands, Astronaut), DeLaval (Sweden, VMS) and GEA (Germany)
  - One robot handles about 60 cows. Robots collect data for each cow: milk yield, milk composition, body weight, steps and more
  - Some farms use robots with both housing and grazing, changing which paddock is open according to when the cows come to the robot

![A milking robot (Lely Astronaut). It finds the teats with a laser](images/milking-robot.jpg)

## Milk prices and milk processors

![A milk tanker. It collects milk from the farm's bulk tank](images/milk-tanker.jpg)

- In Japan, designated organizations (such as Hokuren) sell milk collectively, but in Britain, since the national Milk Marketing Board was abolished in 1994, **farmers contract directly with milk processors**
- The main milk processors

| Company | In brief |
| --- | --- |
| [Arla Foods](https://www.arlafoods.co.uk/) | A Danish–Swedish cooperative. British farmers are members too |
| [Müller](https://www.muller.co.uk/) | A German company. Milk and yogurt |
| [First Milk](https://www.firstmilk.co.uk/) | A cooperative of British farmers. Cheese |
| [Saputo Dairy UK](https://uk.saputo.com/) | A Canadian company. Cheese (Cathedral City) |

- **Milk prices differ by company and contract**, and go up and down almost every month, because they are pulled by world prices for butter and skimmed milk powder
  - In 2026, the price fell to about 34 pence per L in April and recovered to about 37 pence in August
- "**Aligned contracts**" with supermarkets (Tesco, Sainsbury's and others) set prices based on production costs, so they are stable and relatively high. In return, the conditions on animal welfare and the environment are strict
- Many farms hold **Red Tractor** certification (Britain's food safety and quality assurance scheme)

## Current worries

### Bovine TB and badgers

![A badger. Considered a wild animal that spreads bovine TB, it has long been the subject of debate](images/badger.jpg)

- **Bovine tuberculosis** (bTB) is the biggest worry for dairy farmers in southwest England and Wales. When a cow tests positive, it is culled, and the farm is stopped from moving cattle off
- Wild **badgers** are believed to spread it, and England culled badgers from 2013. Animal protection groups strongly opposed it, and the debate split the country
- By July 2026, all cull licenses had ended, and from now on TB will be reduced by stricter cattle testing and vaccinating badgers. The share of herds with TB fell to 3.3% in England in March 2026, the lowest since 2007. The goal is **a TB-free England by 2038**

### Prices, labor and successors

- When feed, fertilizer and electricity prices rise and milk prices fall, farms quickly go into the red
- There are not enough people to care for cows and milk them. Many foreign workers are employed
- The number of young heifers joining herds is falling, raising worries that milk output will fall in future

### Environment

- There are strict rules on the size of slurry storage tanks and when slurry can be spread, to keep rivers clean
- Farmers are being asked to reduce **methane** from cows' burps. In 2024, when Arla tested a methane-reducing feed additive (Bovaer), calls to "boycott" its milk spread online. An episode that showed how hard it is to balance environmental action with consumers' feelings

## Sensors and AI

- On large farms, it has become normal to detect heat and illness (a drop in rumination) with sensors on collars, leg bands and ear tags

| Product | Company | Where it goes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [SenseHub](https://msd.sensehub.com/) | MSD Animal Health (originally Israel's SCR) | Neck, ear |
| [CowManager](https://www.cowmanager.com/) | Netherlands | Ear |
| [CowScout](https://www.gea.com/en/products/milking-farming-barn/gea-herd-management/activity-detection-cowscout/) | GEA (Germany) | Neck, leg |
| [CowAlert](https://www.peacocktechnology.com/cowalert) | Peacock Technology (Scotland; bought IceRobotics of Edinburgh in 2022) | Hind leg |
| [Moocall](https://www.moocall.com/) | Ireland | Tail (calving alerts) |

- **Cameras and AI**: cameras above barn passageways automatically score gait (**lameness**) and body condition (BCS) every day. Companies include [**CattleEye**](https://cattleeye.com/) in Belfast (a GEA company since 2024; GEA opened a software lab in Belfast in March 2026). In a 2026 survey, the technology farmers were "most interested in" was **camera AI**, ahead of milking robots
- British milk processors and supermarkets often require regular recording of **mobility scores** (lameness scores), so there is demand for scoring them automatically
