## "The UK" is four countries

The official name is the **United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland**, usually called the **UK**. The Japanese name *Igirisu* comes from the Portuguese *inglês* ("English"), and is used only in Japan.

![The four countries of the UK. Red is England, blue is Scotland, green is Wales and yellow is Northern Ireland (grey is the Republic of Ireland)](images/uk-countries-map.jpg)

| Country | Main city | Population (around 2025) | In brief |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| England | London | About 58.8 million | Over 80% of the population. The southeast is flat with lots of arable land |
| Scotland | Edinburgh | About 5.5 million | Northern highlands and islands. Whisky, sheep, beef, salmon |
| Wales | Cardiff | About 3.2 million | Mountains and grassland. Sheep and dairy. Welsh is also an official language |
| Northern Ireland | Belfast | About 1.9 million | The northeast of the island of Ireland. Much grassland; dairy and beef cattle |

- **Great Britain** (GB) is the large island with England, Scotland and Wales. It does not include Northern Ireland. This is why statistics separate "GB" and "UK"
- In football and rugby, the four countries play as separate teams
- Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland each have their own **parliament and government**, and decide things like agriculture, education and health themselves (**devolution**). So **the systems of farm subsidies differ between the four countries**

## The UK in numbers

| Item | Figure | For comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Area | About 244,000 km² | About two-thirds of Japan |
| Population | About 69.5 million (mid-2025) | A little more than half of Japan |
| Farmland | About 17 million ha (about 70% of the land) | About four times Japan's farmland (about 4.3 million ha) |
| Capital | London (about 9 million) | One of the largest cities in Europe |
| Currency | Pound (£, GBP). £1 = 100 pence (p) | In November 2026, £1 was about 210 yen |
| Time difference | 9 hours behind Japan (8 hours in summer time) | 5 p.m. in Japan is 8 a.m. in London (9 a.m. in summer) |

- The population has been growing because many people come from abroad. But in 2025, immigration fell sharply, and growth was the smallest since 2020
- Milk prices are talked about in "pence per litre" (**ppl**)

## How the country is run

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Form of state | **Constitutional monarchy**. There is a king, but politics is run by Parliament and the Cabinet |
| King | Charles III (September 2022–) |
| Parliament | The **House of Commons** (lower house, 650 members, elected) and the **House of Lords** (upper house, not elected) |
| Prime Minister | Andy Burnham (Labour, July 2026–) |
| Main parties | Labour (center-left), Conservatives (center-right), Liberal Democrats, Reform UK (right-wing, led by people who pushed for leaving the EU), Scottish National Party (SNP), Plaid Cymru (Wales) |

- There is no single written constitution. Laws, court precedents and conventions together take its place
- Elections use single-member constituencies (only one person wins in each), which favors large parties
- The UK left the EU (European Union) in 2020 (**Brexit**). See Chapters 2 and 5 for details

## Landforms

![Glen Coe in the Scottish Highlands. The north and west have many mountains and grasslands](images/glen-coe.jpg)

- Roughly speaking, the UK is "**low and flat in the southeast, high in the north and west**"
  - **The east and southeast** (East Anglia, Lincolnshire and others): flat with little rain → fields of wheat, barley, oilseed rape, sugar beet and potatoes
  - **The west and north** (Wales, northern England, Scotland): hills and mountains, lots of rain → cattle and sheep on grassland
- The highest mountain is Ben Nevis in Scotland (1,345 m). Not even half the height of Mount Fuji
- On the coast, the White Cliffs of Dover (chalk, a limestone rock) in the southeast are famous. The Strait of Dover is about 34 km wide at its narrowest, and you can see France

## Climate

- A **temperate oceanic climate**. Thanks to a warm ocean current (the North Atlantic Current), winters are not very cold even though the UK lies farther north than Hokkaido. London's average January temperature is about 5–6 °C
- It "rains often, but not much". London gets about 600 mm of rain a year, less than half of Tokyo (about 1,500 mm). Instead, there are many cloudy days with light rain
- The west (Wales, western Scotland) is rainy, with over 2,000 mm a year in some places. Grass grows well, which suits pasture and dairy
- It lies at a high latitude (London is at 51°N, about the same as northern Sakhalin), so in summer it stays light until after 9 p.m., and in winter it is dark by 4 p.m.
- Hot summers and droughts are becoming more common. In 2022 the temperature passed 40 °C for the first time, and in the **summer drought of 2025** harvests of wheat and other crops fell sharply

## Language and religion

- The language is **English**. Welsh (spoken by about 20%), Scottish Gaelic, Irish and others are also used. In Wales, road signs are written in two languages, English and Welsh
- Even within English, pronunciation and vocabulary differ a lot by region (Chapter 4)
- The state church is the **Church of England** (Christian). The king is its head. However, few people go to church every week, and more and more people answer that they have "no religion"
- Large cities such as London and Birmingham also have many Muslims, Hindus and Sikhs
