# 6. Farming in the UK

## What you will learn

- British agriculture in numbers (compared with Japan)
- Arable farming in the east, livestock in the west and north
- The main crops (wheat, barley, oilseed rape, sugar beet, potatoes) and sheep
- After EU subsidies (SFI), and the inheritance tax row
- Current worries (drought, labor, money)

## In numbers

| Item | UK | Japan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Farmland | About 17 million ha (about 70% of the land) | About 4.3 million ha (about 10% of the land) |
| Size of one farm | About 80 ha on average | About 3 ha on average (about 30 ha in Hokkaido) |
| Total farm income (2025) | About £8.4 billion (about 1.8 trillion yen) | ― |
| Food self-sufficiency (by value, 2025) | 60% (72% for foods that can be grown in the UK) | 64% (production value basis) |

- About two-thirds of farmland is **grassland** (pasture and upland grazing), and about one-third is arable. Rain is plentiful and grass grows well, which suits keeping cattle and sheep
- Farm income in 2025 rose 21% from the previous year. That was thanks to good prices for beef and milk; arable farming (wheat and others) suffered from drought and low prices. People said "**up horn, down corn**"
- Self-sufficiency fell from 65% in 2024 to 60%. This was because the drought reduced harvests and the price of imported food rose. More than half of vegetables and fruit are imported (94% of carrots are home-grown)

## Arable in the east, livestock in the west and north

| Region | Main farming |
| --- | --- |
| Eastern England (East Anglia, Lincolnshire, East Yorkshire) | Wheat, barley, oilseed rape, sugar beet, potatoes, vegetables. Flat with little rain. "Britain's breadbasket" |
| Southwest England (Devon, Somerset, Cornwall), Cheshire | Dairy. Lots of rain, and grass grows well |
| Wales, the Lake District, the Pennines, the Scottish Highlands | Grazing sheep and beef cattle. Hilly and unsuited to arable crops |
| Northern Ireland | Grass-based dairy and beef cattle |
| Kent, Herefordshire | Fruit (apples, strawberries, hops) |

- Field boundaries have **hedgerows** (living fences made by planting shrubs) or, in the north and west, **dry stone walls** (walls of stacked stone without mortar). Many date from the enclosures of the 18th century (Chapter 2), and today they are protected with subsidies as homes for birds and insects

![Dry stone walls in the Yorkshire Dales. They divide the grassland and keep sheep from escaping](images/dry-stone-walls.jpg)

## Main crops

### Wheat

![Harvesting wheat. Grain is moved from the combine into a trailer](images/combine-wheat.jpg)

- The UK's biggest crop (the largest by crop sales in 2025, about £2.1 billion)
- Almost all is **winter wheat** (sown in September–October, overwintered and harvested the next August)
- About **8 tonnes** per hectare. That is 1.5–2 times Japanese wheat (4–5 tonnes), and one of the highest yields in the world, because the cool summers let the grain ripen slowly
- There is wheat for bread, and wheat for livestock feed, whisky and ethanol
- In 2025, because of drought, wheat self-sufficiency (by volume) was 83% (in a normal year the UK covers its own needs and exports a little)

### Barley

- For malt for beer and whisky, and for livestock feed
- Scottish barley is an important raw material for Scotch whisky (Chapter 3)

### Oilseed rape

![A field of oilseed rape in flower in spring. Around May, the countryside turns yellow](images/oilseed-rape.jpg)

- Used for oil (canola oil), with the pressed meal used as livestock feed
- Important as a crop in the wheat rotation
- Neonicotinoid insecticides were banned in the EU era, damage from flea beetles increased, and the area grown fell sharply

### Sugar beet, potatoes and vegetables

![Harvesting sugar beet (Norfolk). It is piled beside the field and taken to the sugar factory](images/sugar-beet.jpg)

- **Sugar beet**: grown only in eastern England and made into sugar in the factories of a single company, British Sugar. Harvested from autumn into winter
- **Potatoes**: much is for chips and crisps. Scotland produces disease-free **seed potatoes**
- **Vegetables**: carrots, cabbages, leeks, Brussels sprouts and others. In Lincolnshire and Norfolk

## Sheep

![Herdwick sheep in the Lake District. Hardy against cold and rain, they live on the fells all year](images/herdwick.jpg)

- The UK has about 30 million sheep — about half its human population. One of the largest numbers in Europe
- The sheep's job is to turn hill and mountain grassland (land that in Japan would just be empty mountains) into food (lamb) and wool
- There are **more than 60 breeds**, differing by region. Hardy breeds are raised in the hills (Herdwick, Swaledale, Welsh Mountain) and crossed with meatier breeds in the lowlands, a flow from hill to lowland
- About a third of lamb is exported (especially to the EU, such as France). It is a typical industry where Brexit increased the burden of checks
- Wool is cheap today, and shearing often costs more than the wool is worth

![Mountain sheep in Wales. The marks on their bodies show the owner](images/snowdonia-sheep.jpg)

## After EU subsidies

- While in the EU, farmers received subsidies under the **CAP** according to farmland area (the Basic Payment Scheme, BPS). The bigger the farm, the more it got
- After leaving the EU, each of the four countries decides its own farm policy
- **England**: area payments (BPS) have been cut step by step since 2021 and will end in 2027. Instead, "**Environmental Land Management**" (ELM) has begun
  - **SFI** (Sustainable Farming Incentive): choose and commit to "actions" such as protecting soil, maintaining hedges, reducing pesticides or mixing flowers into grassland, and get paid for them
  - Countryside Stewardship (larger commitments to protect nature)
  - Landscape Recovery (restoring woods and wetlands across wide areas)
- SFI applications exceeded the budget and were suddenly closed in March 2025, angering farmers. It reopened in 2026 as the new **SFI26** (actions cut from 102 to 71, a cap of £100,000 a year per farm, and two application windows, in June and September)
- **Wales** started its "Sustainable Farming Scheme" in 2026, and **Scotland** keeps area payments but attaches conditions; each country does things differently

## The inheritance tax row

![Tractors lined up in Whitehall, London's government district, in a protest against the government](images/tractors-whitehall.jpg)

- Until now, farmland was **100% exempt** from inheritance tax when inherited (Agricultural Property Relief, APR)
- In October 2024, the government announced it would change this to "exempt up to £1 million, with 20% tax on anything above". The reason given was to stop wealthy people buying farmland to avoid tax
- British farmland costs around £20,000 per hectare, so even a family farm of about 100 ha goes over the limit. Saying "we will have to sell the farm to pay the tax", farmers drove tractors into London and protested many times (**No Farmers, No Food**)
- The government revised the plan in December 2025, and **from April 2026 the limit is £2.5 million per person** (£5 million for a couple). This greatly reduced the number of farms affected

## Current worries

- **Weather**: 2024 was too wet to sow, and 2025 brought drought. Changing weather makes harvests swing widely from year to year
- **Money**: the costs of fertilizer, fuel, machinery and labor are rising, while crop prices are set by world markets, so profits are hard to make
- **Labor**: harvesting fruit and vegetables used to rely on people from Eastern Europe. Since Brexit, workers also come from Central Asia and elsewhere on the "**Seasonal Worker visa**". Labor shortages are a reason for interest in robots (Chapter 8)
- **Aging farmers**: the average age of farm holders is about 60. Some farms have no one to take over
- **Environment**: river pollution (livestock manure and fertilizer), declining wildlife, greenhouse gases. Environmental demands get stricter every year

## Farmers' organizations and research

- **NFU** (National Farmers' Union): the large farmers' organization for England and Wales. It lobbies the government and puts out news
- **AHDB**: an organization that uses money collected from farmers for market information and research. It publishes statistics such as milk prices and cattle numbers
- There are many places for agricultural research and education, such as **Rothamsted Research** (Chapter 8), Harper Adams University and SRUC (Scotland)
