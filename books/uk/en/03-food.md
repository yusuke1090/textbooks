# 3. Food

People often say "British food is bad", but the ingredients (beef, lamb, dairy products, potatoes) are good, and restaurant standards have risen recently. Home cooking is mainly simple: roasting, boiling and frying.

## Meals of the day

| Meal | Commonly eaten |
| --- | --- |
| Breakfast | Cereal with milk, toast, tea. A full breakfast on days off |
| Lunch | Sandwiches, soup, crisps. Many people keep lunch light |
| Dinner (also called tea) | Meat or fish with potatoes and vegetables. Pasta and curry are normal too |

- In the north of England and elsewhere, some people call their evening meal "**tea**". A very British example of words differing by region and class
- Supermarkets (Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda, Morrisons, Aldi, Lidl) have very well-developed sandwiches and ready meals

## The full breakfast

![A full English breakfast: fried egg, bacon, sausage, baked beans, tomato and more](images/full-english.jpg)

- Fried eggs, bacon, sausages, **baked beans** (white beans in tomato sauce), grilled tomatoes and mushrooms, toast, and black pudding (a pork blood sausage)
- In Scotland it comes with **haggis**, and in Northern Ireland with potato bread (potato farl)
- It is not eaten every morning; it is a treat for days off, at hotels and cafes

## Fish and chips

![Fish and chips (Blackpool)](images/fish-and-chips.jpg)

- Cod or haddock fried in batter, with thick-cut fried potatoes (**chips**; called French fries in America)
- Sprinkled with salt and malt vinegar, and served with mashed green peas (mushy peas)
- In the 19th century, when railways began bringing fish quickly from the ports, it spread as a cheap meal for factory workers
- **Chips** are fried potatoes, and **crisps** are potato chips. Be careful, as this is different from American English

## The Sunday roast

![A Sunday roast: roast beef, Yorkshire pudding, roast potatoes and vegetables, covered in gravy](images/sunday-roast.jpg)

- A special meal eaten on Sunday at lunchtime, at home with the family or at the pub
- Roast meat (beef, lamb, pork or chicken), roast potatoes, boiled vegetables (carrots, Brussels sprouts, peas) and **Yorkshire pudding** (a batter of flour, eggs and milk puffed up in the oven)
- Plenty of **gravy**, made from the meat juices, is poured over it
- Beef comes with horseradish, and lamb with mint sauce

## Tea and afternoon tea

![A cream tea: scones with clotted cream and jam, with milk tea](images/cream-tea.jpg)

- British people drink many cups of tea a day each. Strong tea from a tea bag **with milk** is normal. "Let's have a cuppa" is almost a greeting for a break at work
- **Afternoon tea**: sandwiches (cucumber and others), scones and cakes on a three-tiered stand. A special treat eaten at hotels
- **Cream tea**: scones with **clotted cream** (a thick, rich cream) and jam, eaten with tea. Its home is Devon and Cornwall in the southwest (dairy regions), and the two counties arguing over "cream first or jam first" is a classic joke
- Tea comes from India, Sri Lanka, Kenya and elsewhere. Much of Kenya's tea is sold to Britain (see the Africa Textbook)

## Pubs

![A country pub. It is also a meeting place for the village](images/pub.jpg)

- A **pub** (public house) is a place to drink, where you can also eat. In villages, it is a "gathering place" alongside the church
- You order at the bar and pay on the spot (staff often do not come to your table). In groups, there is the custom of the "**round**", where each person takes a turn buying drinks for everyone
- The traditional beer is unchilled **ale** (bitter). Cold lager and apple-based **cider** are also popular
- One pint (about 568 mL) is the unit for one glass. Beer and milk are still counted in pints
- Pubs are hard to keep running, and their number falls every year

## Cheese

![Cheddar cheese (Somerset)](images/cheddar.jpg)

- **Cheddar**: named after the village of Cheddar in Somerset, southwest England. One of the most produced cheeses in the world. More than half of the cheese eaten in Britain is cheddar
- When making it, the curds (set milk) are stacked and turned over and over to drain the whey, a process called "**cheddaring**"
- There are more than 700 other cheeses named after places, such as Stilton (blue mold), Wensleydale, Red Leicester and Lancashire
- About a quarter of milk becomes cheese. Cheese makers are important buyers of milk (Chapter 7)

## Food of Scotland and Wales

![Haggis, neeps and tatties (Scotland), served with mashed turnip and potato](images/haggis.jpg)

- **Haggis**: sheep's offal (heart, liver, lungs) mixed with oats, onions and spices, stuffed into a sheep's stomach and boiled. Served with mashed turnip (neeps) and potato (tatties). Eaten on January 25, the birthday of the poet Robert Burns (Burns Night)
- **Oats**: in Scotland, where it is too cold for wheat to grow well, people have long eaten oat porridge (**porridge**)
- **Wales**: lamb (Welsh lamb), leeks (the national symbol), and "Welsh rarebit", melted cheese on toast
- **Salmon**: Scottish farmed salmon is Britain's largest food export by value

## Whisky

![The copper stills (pot stills) of a Scotch whisky distillery](images/whisky-stills.jpg)

- **Scotch whisky** is made from malted barley. Only whisky distilled in Scotland and aged in wooden casks for 3 years or more can be called "Scotch"
- How it is made: sprout the barley (malt) → dry it (burning peat at this stage gives a smoky smell) → dissolve it in hot water to make sugar → ferment with yeast → distill twice in copper stills → age in casks
- It is a big buyer of Scottish barley, and a major pillar of exports
- Masataka Taketsuru, who founded Nikka Whisky, studied in Scotland from 1918 to 1920 and returned to Japan with his Scottish wife, Rita

## Food brought by immigrants

![Chicken tikka masala. A curry said to have been born in Britain](images/tikka-masala.jpg)

- **Curry**: there are many restaurants run by people from India, Pakistan and Bangladesh, and curry is now a national dish of Britain. **Chicken tikka masala** is said to be a dish born in Britain
- Chinese takeaways, Turkish kebabs and Polish grocery stores are also common
- Recently, Japanese food is popular too. Supermarkets sell sushi, and there are chains selling ramen and katsu curry (so much so that in Britain "katsu curry" is used to mean a curry-flavored sauce)
