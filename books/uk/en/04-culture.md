Many of the sports played around the world today — football, rugby, cricket, tennis, golf and others — got their rules in 19th-century Britain.

## Sports

![Wembley Stadium in London, the home of the England team](images/wembley.jpg)

- **Football** (soccer): the most popular. England's **Premier League** is the league with the most money in the world, and there are many Japanese players. Families support their local team for generations
- **Rugby**: the national sport in Wales. It was born at an English school (Rugby School). Every spring there is the Six Nations tournament
- **Cricket**: a summer sport. Some matches (Test matches) last five days. Local teams playing on a village green at the weekend is a symbol of the English summer
- Also Wimbledon (tennis), St Andrews in Scotland (the home of golf), and horse racing (much loved by the royal family too)

![A village cricket match. Players wear white, with a break for tea](images/cricket.jpg)

## Music, literature and theater

- **Music**: the Beatles (Liverpool), the Rolling Stones, Queen, Oasis, Ed Sheeran, Adele and more. Britain is a pop music superpower alongside America
- **Literature**: Shakespeare (16th–17th centuries), Jane Austen, Dickens, Sherlock Holmes (Conan Doyle), Harry Potter (J. K. Rowling, written in Edinburgh), and Nobel Prize winner Kazuo Ishiguro (born in Nagasaki)
- **Theater**: London's West End is a center of musicals and theater, alongside New York's Broadway
- Many national museums and galleries, such as the British Museum and the National Gallery, have **free admission**

## Festivals

- **Notting Hill Carnival** (end of August, London): a festival of people who came from the Caribbean. One of the largest street festivals in Europe
- **Guy Fawkes Night** (November 5): a day to remember the 1605 plot to blow up the Houses of Parliament. Fireworks and bonfires
- **Christmas**: the biggest event of the year. Families eat turkey and watch the King's speech. The next day, December 26, is a holiday called "Boxing Day"
- In summer, **agricultural shows** are held all over the country: cattle and sheep judging, displays of tractors and machinery, stalls of local food. Townspeople come with their families to enjoy them too (Chapter 9)

## Customs and manners

- **Queuing**: taken very seriously. Cutting in line is strongly disliked
- **"Sorry", "Please" and "Thank you"**: used so often that even the person who gets bumped into says "sorry". When getting off a bus, people say "Cheers!" (thanks) to the driver
- **Talking about the weather**: even with strangers, start with the weather. It is a way to begin a conversation
- **Indirect speech**: people say things softly rather than directly. "That's interesting" can really mean "I don't agree". "Not bad" is quite high praise
- **Humor**: people love irony and self-deprecating jokes. Even at work, people who can make jokes are liked
- **Class**: awareness of "class" — working, middle and upper — still remains a little, and shows in accent, schools, food and taste in sports
- **Driving on the left**, with right-hand-drive cars. The same as Japan
- **Electricity**: 230 V, with plugs that have three rectangular pins (type BF). Japanese electrical goods need an adapter and a check that they work at that voltage

## British English

- Words and spellings differ from American English

| British | American | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| flat | apartment | An apartment |
| lift | elevator | An elevator |
| lorry | truck | A truck |
| petrol | gas | Gasoline |
| queue | line | A line of people |
| mobile | cell phone | A mobile phone |
| colour, centre, organise | color, center, organize | Spelling |
| first floor | second floor | The 2nd story (the British 1st story is the ground floor) |

- Pronunciation varies a lot by region: London's East End (Cockney), Liverpool (Scouse), Newcastle (Geordie), Scotland, Northern Ireland and so on. Talking with farmers takes some getting used to regional speech
- You often hear "Cheers" (thanks, or a toast), "Mate" (friend, you), "Brilliant" (great), "Lovely" (nice) and "Fancy a cuppa?" (how about a cup of tea?)

## The look of the towns

![London's red double-decker bus](images/routemaster.jpg)

![The roundel symbol of the Underground (the Tube)](images/underground.jpg)

![A red phone box. Many are no longer used and have been turned into AED stations or small libraries](images/phone-box.jpg)

- **The London Underground** (opened in 1863) is the oldest underground railway in the world. It is called "the Tube". You ride by tapping a card or phone
- Red double-decker buses and black taxis (black cabs) are symbols of London
- Old brick houses are normal. People live in houses more than 100 years old, repairing them as they go. The older the house, the more popular it is
- People love their gardens, and gardening is a national hobby
