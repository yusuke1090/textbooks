# 5. Trends today

This chapter is based on research done in November 2026. Politics moves fast, so check the latest news before relying on it.

## Politics: two prime ministers in two years

![The black door of the Prime Minister's residence (10 Downing Street)](images/downing-street.jpg)

- **The general election of July 2024**: the Conservative government, in power for 14 years, lost, and Labour won a landslide. Keir Starmer became prime minister
- After that, rising prices and tax increases made the government less popular, and **Reform UK** (a right-wing party led by Nigel Farage, tough on immigration and a driver of Brexit) rose in the polls
- In the **May 2026 elections**, Labour suffered heavy losses
  - **Scotland**: the SNP (Scottish National Party) was the largest party with 58 of 129 seats. First Minister John Swinney stayed on. The number of pro-independence members was the highest ever
  - **Wales**: Labour, the largest party in the Senedd since 1999, fell to 9 seats, and **Plaid Cymru** (the Welsh party) became the largest party with 43 of 96 seats. Reform UK was second with 34 seats. Rhun ap Iorwerth became the first First Minister of Wales from outside Labour
- Within Labour, discontent with Prime Minister Starmer grew, and **Andy Burnham**, the former Mayor of Manchester, became an MP in a by-election in June (Makerfield)
- Starmer resigned, and Burnham became Labour leader on July 17 and **prime minister on July 20**. He is the seventh prime minister in ten years
- When a party changes its leader, the prime minister changes without a general election (the same as when Japan's LDP elects a new president)

## Living costs and money

- In 2022, Russia's invasion of Ukraine pushed up the prices of gas and food, and inflation rose above 11% a year (the **cost of living crisis**). It has calmed down now, but food and rents remain high
- Economic growth is weak and national debt is high, so the government is always struggling over how to raise taxes and cut spending
- The minimum wage (the National Living Wage for those 21 and over) is £12.71 an hour from April 2026 (about 2,600 yen). The cost of farm labor is also rising (Chapter 6)
- The number of people coming from abroad to live (net migration) passed 900,000 a year around 2023, but after visas were tightened it fell to about 200,000 in 2025

## Resetting relations with the EU

- After leaving the EU, food trade required many inspections and health certificates. Some small companies gave up selling to the EU
- So the government has been negotiating an **SPS agreement** with the EU (a commitment to align health rules for food, animals and plants). In November 2026 it was reported that an agreement in principle had been reached, and it is expected to be discussed at a summit with the EU on November 20
- In return for following EU rules (dynamic alignment), most border checks are expected to disappear. It would start in mid-2027 at the earliest, and some expect it in 2028 in practice
- For farmers and food companies, it will be easier to sell to the EU, but there are also worries that Britain will no longer be able to set its own rules on things like pesticides and gene editing

## Energy: ending coal, moving to offshore wind

![An offshore wind farm (off Barrow, northwest England). The UK's offshore wind capacity is second in the world after China](images/offshore-wind.jpg)

- Britain, the country of the Industrial Revolution, closed **its last coal-fired power station** (Ratcliffe-on-Soar) **on September 30, 2024**. It was the first G7 country to do so
- Using the shallow, windy North Sea, it has greatly expanded **offshore wind**. About 30% of the year's electricity comes from wind
- A goal of bringing greenhouse gas emissions to "net zero" by 2050 is set in law. Agriculture is also being asked to cut methane from cattle burps and emissions from fertilizer (Chapter 7)

## London as a tech city

![Canary Wharf in London, where banks and fintech companies are concentrated](images/canary-wharf.jpg)

- London is the city in Europe that attracts the most startups and investment
- **Fintech** (finance and technology): the mobile banks Revolut and Monzo, the international transfer company Wise and others
- **AI**: Google DeepMind is based in London. The government also set up an institute for AI safety research
- **Semiconductors**: **Arm** (Cambridge), whose smartphone CPU designs are used in almost every phone in the world, is mostly owned by Japan's SoftBank Group
- In 2022, the **Elizabeth line**, a new railway connecting London from east to west, opened

![Inside an Elizabeth line train. Station names are shown on electronic displays](images/elizabeth-line.jpg)

## Farming news (details in Chapters 6–7)

- **Inheritance tax**: in October 2024, the government announced that it would reduce inheritance tax relief on farmland, and farmers drove tractors into London to protest ("No Farmers, No Food"). The government revised the plan in December 2025, and from April 2026 the limit for 100% relief is £2.5 million per person (£5 million for a couple)
- **Switching subsidies**: ending EU-style subsidies based on area, and moving to a system that pays for what farmers do that is good for the environment (SFI and others). Applications for the new SFI opened in 2026
- **The end of badger culling**: the badger culling carried out to reduce bovine tuberculosis (bovine TB) in cattle ended in 2026. From now on, TB will be reduced by testing cattle and vaccinating badgers
- **Drought**: harvests fell in the 2025 drought, and food self-sufficiency (calculated by value) fell to 60%
